
## Experiment Draft

### Experimental Purpose

The primary goal of this experiment is to maximize flux pinning in multi-element-doped REBCO (Rare Earth Barium Copper Oxide) films through the introduction of mixed phase additions, thereby enhancing the critical current density (Jc) across a broad range of temperatures and magnetic fields. Additionally, the experiment aims to maintain a high critical temperature (Tc) and structural stability in the films. The target sample dimensions are 22–25 mm in diameter and 2–3 mm in thickness. This research will explore the synergistic effects of multi-element rare-earth co-doping, hybrid artificial pinning centers (APCs), and advanced texture and stress tuning strategies to achieve superior superconducting properties.

### Experimental Materials

#### Raw Materials:

  1. **REBCO Precursors** : Y₂O₃, Yb₂O₃, Sm₂O₃, BaCO₃, CuO. 
  2. **Doping Materials** : BaZrO₃ (BZO), Ba₂YNbO₆ (BYNO), ZrO₂, Y₂O₃, CuO. 
  3. **Buffer Layer Materials** : SrTiO₃ (STO), LaMnO₃ (LMO), CeO₂. 
  4. **Substrate** : Single-crystal SrTiO₃ (100) with nanoimprint lithography patterning. 
  5. **Gases** : High-purity O₂ for annealing and PLD deposition. 



#### Experimental Instruments:

  1. **Pulsed Laser Deposition (PLD) System** : For thin film deposition with bidirectional PLD capability. 
  2. **High-Temperature Furnace** : For sintering PLD targets and annealing films. 
  3. **Nanoimprint Lithography System** : For substrate patterning. 
  4. **X-ray Diffractometer (XRD)** : For structural and texture analysis. 
  5. **Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM)** : For defect and interface characterization. 
  6. **Atomic Force Microscope (AFM)** : For surface morphology analysis. 
  7. **Superconducting Property Measurement System (PPMS)** : For Tc and Jc measurements. 
  8. **Raman Spectrometer** : For stress and phase analysis. 



### Experimental Procedures

#### 1\. **Target Preparation and Defect Introduction Strategy**

  * **Step 1** : Synthesize the multi-element REBCO target with the formula (Y₀.₇Yb₀.₂Sm₀.₁)Ba₂Cu₃O₇-δ by solid-state reaction. 
  * **Step 2** : Introduce 5 vol% BaZrO₃ (BZO) and 2 vol% Ba₂YNbO₆ (BYNO) into the target for hybrid APC formation. 
  * **Step 3** : Pre-dope 1 wt% ZrO₂ nanoparticles into the target for grain boundary segregation. 
  * **Step 4** : Sinter the target at 950°C for 12 hours in an O₂ atmosphere. 



#### 2\. **Substrate and Buffer Layer Preparation**

  * **Step 1** : Pattern the SrTiO₃ substrate with 50 nm-period grooves along the [010] direction using nanoimprint lithography. 
  * **Step 2** : Deposit a lattice-matched buffer layer stack (STO/LMO/CeO₂) via PLD, ensuring stepwise lattice transition. 
  * **Step 3** : Optimize the compressive strain in the LaMnO₃ layer by controlling thickness (30 nm) and O₂ pressure (200 mTorr). 



#### 3\. **Thin Film Deposition**

  * **Step 1** : Deposit the REBCO film using bidirectional PLD with alternating pulse frequencies (10 Hz/2 Hz every 100 nm). 
  * **Step 2** : Apply a substrate bias of -50 V DC during PLD to counteract intrinsic tensile stress. 
  * **Step 3** : Perform gradient annealing (400°C→600°C at 5°C/min) with O₂ pressure increasing from 0.1 to 1 atm. 



#### 4\. **Characterization and Testing**

  * **Step 1** : Analyze film structure and texture using XRD and TEM. 
  * **Step 2** : Characterize defect morphology and distribution using AFM and TEM. 
  * **Step 3** : Measure Tc and Jc using PPMS across various temperatures and magnetic fields. 
  * **Step 4** : Evaluate stress and strain using Raman spectroscopy. 



### Precautions

#### 1\. **Target Preparation**

  * **Factors** : Ionic size mismatch, phase purity, and homogeneity of dopants. 
  * **Impact** : Inhomogeneous doping can lead to inconsistent defect distribution, affecting flux pinning. 
  * **Testing** : XRD and TEM to confirm phase purity and dopant distribution. 



#### 2\. **Substrate and Buffer Layer**

  * **Factors** : Lattice mismatch, strain, and interfacial defects. 
  * **Impact** : Poor lattice matching can introduce defects, degrading superconducting properties. 
  * **Testing** : XRD and TEM for lattice parameter and interface analysis. 



#### 3\. **Thin Film Deposition**

  * **Factors** : Deposition rate, pulse frequency, and O₂ pressure. 
  * **Impact** : Improper deposition parameters can lead to poor texture and stress accumulation. 
  * **Testing** : AFM and Raman spectroscopy for surface morphology and stress analysis. 



#### 4\. **Characterization and Testing**

  * **Factors** : Defect morphology, pinning strength, and Jc. 
  * **Impact** : Inadequate defect characterization can lead to misinterpretation of pinning mechanisms. 
  * **Testing** : TEM and PPMS for defect and Jc analysis. 



### Expected Results

  1. **Structural Properties** : High-quality REBCO films with aligned BZO nanocolumns and isotropic BYNO nanoparticles. 
  2. **Superconducting Properties** : Tc > 90 K and enhanced Jc across a broad range of temperatures and magnetic fields. 
  3. **Defect Morphology** : Dense point-like defects and well-aligned nanocolumns for effective flux pinning. 
  4. **Stress and Strain** : Residual stress < 200 MPa and strain ε < 0.1%, ensuring structural stability. 
  5. **Current-Carrying Performance** : Superior Jc performance due to optimized pinning centers and texture. 



This experiment is expected to provide a comprehensive understanding of the interplay between composition, texture, and stress in enhancing the superconducting properties of REBCO films, paving the way for advanced applications in high-field magnets and energy systems.

# Experimental Steps

## Experimental Steps1

**Experimental Step 1: Synthesis of Multi-Element REBCO Target via Solid-State Reaction**

**Experimental Goal** :  
To synthesize a multi-element REBCO target with the formula (Y₀.₇Yb₀.₂Sm₀.₁)Ba₂Cu₃O₇-δ using a solid-state reaction method, ensuring precise stoichiometry, phase purity, and minimal carbon content for subsequent thin film deposition.

**Experimental Materials** :  
\- Precursor Powders: Y₂O₃ (99.99% purity), Yb₂O₃ (99.99% purity), Sm₂O₃ (99.99% purity), BaCO₃ (99.99% purity), CuO (99.99% purity).  
\- Binder: Polyvinyl Alcohol (PVA, 2% solution).  
\- Solvent: Ethanol (99.8% purity). 

**Experimental Instruments** :  
\- High-energy ball mill (zirconia media).  
\- Uniaxial press (400 MPa capacity).  
\- Cold isostatic press (200 MPa capacity).  
\- High-temperature furnace (up to 1000°C, oxygen atmosphere control).  
\- X-ray Diffractometer (XRD, PANalytical X'Pert PRO).  
\- Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM, FEI Nova NanoSEM 450). 

**Experimental Procedure** : 

**Step 1: Precursor Preparation**  
1\. Weigh the precursor powders accurately using an analytical balance to achieve the stoichiometric ratio:  
\- Y₂O₃: 0.7 mol  
\- Yb₂O₃: 0.2 mol  
\- Sm₂O₃: 0.1 mol  
\- BaCO₃: 2 mol  
\- CuO: 3 mol  
2\. Mix the powders thoroughly in a mortar and pestle for 30 minutes to ensure homogeneity. 

**Step 2: Ball Milling**  
1\. Transfer the mixed powders into a high-energy ball mill with zirconia media.  
2\. Add ethanol as a solvent (50% of the powder volume) to prevent agglomeration.  
3\. Mill the mixture at 300 rpm for 12 hours to achieve a particle size distribution of D50 < 1 μm.  
4\. Dry the milled powder in an oven at 80°C for 6 hours. 

**Step 3: Calcination**  
1\. Place the dried powder in an alumina crucible.  
2\. Heat the powder in a high-temperature furnace under flowing oxygen (1 L/min) with the following thermal profile:  
\- Ramp at 5°C/min to 850°C.  
\- Hold at 850°C for 6 hours.  
\- Cool at 2°C/min to room temperature.  
3\. Grind the calcined powder in a mortar and pestle for 15 minutes to break any agglomerates. 

**Step 4: Binder Addition and Pressing**  
1\. Add 2% PVA solution to the calcined powder and mix thoroughly to form a homogeneous paste.  
2\. Uniaxially press the mixture at 400 MPa into a cylindrical pellet (50 mm diameter).  
3\. Perform cold isostatic pressing at 200 MPa to improve density and uniformity. 

**Step 5: Sintering**  
1\. Place the pressed pellet in an alumina crucible.  
2\. Sinter in a high-temperature furnace under flowing oxygen (1 L/min) with the following thermal profile:  
\- Ramp at 5°C/min to 950°C.  
\- Hold at 950°C for 10 hours.  
\- Cool at 2°C/min to room temperature. 

**Step 6: Characterization**  
1\. Perform XRD analysis to confirm phase purity and crystal structure.  
2\. Use SEM to examine the microstructure and grain size distribution.  
3\. Measure the density of the sintered target using Archimedes' principle. 

**Experimental Precautions** :  
\- Ensure accurate weighing of precursors to maintain stoichiometry.  
\- Monitor the calcination and sintering temperatures to avoid phase impurities.  
\- Use XRD to confirm phase purity after each thermal treatment.  
\- Minimize carbon content by repeated grinding and calcination steps. 

**Expected Outcomes** :  
\- A multi-element REBCO target with the formula (Y₀.₇Yb₀.₂Sm₀.₁)Ba₂Cu₃O₇-δ.  
\- Phase purity confirmed by XRD (no secondary phases detected).  
\- Relative density > 95% of theoretical density.  
\- Average grain size of 2.1 ± 0.3 μm.  
\- Minimal carbon content (< 0.1 wt%).

\- Ramp at 5°C/min to 950°C.  
\- Hold at 950°C for 10 hours.  
\- Cool at 2°C/min to room temperature. 

**Step 5: Characterization**  
1\. Perform XRD analysis to confirm phase purity and the presence of BZO and BYNO in the REBCO matrix.  
2\. Use TEM to examine the distribution and size of APCs, ensuring uniform dispersion and alignment.  
3\. Measure the density of the sintered target using Archimedes' principle. 

**Experimental Precautions** :  
\- Ensure accurate weighing and uniform mixing of BZO and BYNO to avoid inhomogeneous defect distribution.

\- Use TEM to confirm the distribution and alignment of APCs in the target.

## Experimental Steps5

**Experimental Step 5: Patterning the SrTiO₃ Substrate with 50 nm-Period Grooves Along the [010] Direction Using Nanoimprint Lithography**

**Experimental Goal** :  
To create a patterned SrTiO₃ substrate with 50 nm-period grooves aligned along the [010] direction, which will serve as a template for the aligned growth of the REBCO superconducting film. The patterned substrate is expected to enhance flux pinning and improve the critical current density (Jc) of the resulting film.

**Experimental Materials** :  
\- Single-crystal SrTiO₃ (100) substrate (10 mm × 10 mm × 0.5 mm).  
\- Nanoimprint lithography resist (e.g., PMMA, 950k molecular weight).  
\- Developer solution (e.g., MIBK:IPA = 1:3).  
\- Etching solution (e.g., Ar/CF₄ plasma for dry etching). 

**Experimental Instruments** :  
\- Nanoimprint lithography system (e.g., Obducat NIL system).  
\- Atomic Force Microscope (AFM, Bruker Dimension Icon).  
\- Spin coater (e.g., Laurell Technologies WS-650MZ-23NPP).  
\- Plasma etcher (e.g., Oxford Instruments PlasmaPro 80).  
\- Hot plate (for resist baking). 

**Experimental Procedure** : 

**Step 1: Substrate Cleaning**  
1\. Clean the SrTiO₃ substrate using a standard RCA cleaning process:  
\- Immerse the substrate in a 1:1:5 solution of NH₄OH:H₂O₂:H₂O at 75°C for 10 minutes.  
\- Rinse thoroughly with deionized water (DI water).  
\- Immerse the substrate in a 1:1:5 solution of HCl:H₂O₂:H₂O at 75°C for 10 minutes.  
\- Rinse again with DI water and dry with nitrogen gas. 

**Step 2: Resist Application**  
1\. Spin-coat the nanoimprint resist (PMMA) onto the cleaned SrTiO₃ substrate at 3000 rpm for 30 seconds to achieve a uniform thickness of ~100 nm.  
2\. Bake the substrate on a hot plate at 180°C for 2 minutes to remove residual solvent. 

**Step 3: Nanoimprint Lithography**  
1\. Load the substrate into the nanoimprint lithography system.  
2\. Use a silicon mold with a 50 nm-period grating pattern aligned along the [010] direction.  
3\. Perform the imprinting process at 180°C with a pressure of 50 bar for 5 minutes.  
4\. Cool the substrate to room temperature and carefully separate the mold from the substrate. 

**Step 4: Resist Development**  
1\. Immerse the substrate in the developer solution (MIBK:IPA = 1:3) for 60 seconds to remove excess resist.  
2\. Rinse the substrate with IPA and dry with nitrogen gas. 

**Step 5: Pattern Transfer via Etching**  
1\. Load the substrate into the plasma etcher.  
2\. Use Ar/CF₄ plasma with the following parameters:  
\- Power: 100 W.  
\- Pressure: 10 mTorr.  
\- Etching time: 60 seconds.  
3\. Etch the SrTiO₃ substrate to transfer the 50 nm-period pattern into the substrate surface. 

**Step 6: Pattern Verification**  
1\. Use AFM to confirm the periodicity, depth, and alignment of the grooves.  
\- Expected groove depth: 20-30 nm.  
\- Expected groove width: 25-30 nm.  
2\. Ensure the grooves are aligned precisely along the [010] direction. 

**Experimental Precautions** :  
\- Ensure the SrTiO₃ substrate is free of contaminants before resist application to avoid defects in the pattern.  
\- Maintain precise alignment of the mold during the nanoimprint process to ensure the grooves are aligned along the [010] direction.  
\- Optimize the etching parameters to achieve the desired groove depth without over-etching.  
\- Use AFM to verify the pattern quality and alignment before proceeding to film deposition. 

**Expected Outcomes** :  
\- A SrTiO₃ substrate with 50 nm-period grooves aligned along the [010] direction.  
\- Groove depth of 20-30 nm and width of 25-30 nm, as confirmed by AFM.  
\- Precise alignment of the grooves, ensuring optimal conditions for aligned REBCO film growth.  
\- The patterned substrate is ready for subsequent deposition of the REBCO superconducting film.

## Experimental Steps6

**Experimental Step 6: Deposition of a Lattice-Matched Buffer Layer Stack (STO/LMO/CeO₂) via Pulsed Laser Deposition (PLD)**

**Experimental Goal** :  
To deposit a buffer layer stack consisting of SrTiO₃ (STO), LaMnO₃ (LMO), and CeO₂ via PLD, ensuring a stepwise lattice transition from the patterned SrTiO₃ substrate to the REBCO superconducting film. This buffer layer stack aims to minimize lattice mismatch and interfacial defects, thereby enhancing the epitaxial growth and performance of the REBCO film.

**Experimental Materials** :  
\- SrTiO₃ (STO) target (99.99% purity)  
\- LaMnO₃ (LMO) target (99.99% purity)  
\- CeO₂ target (99.99% purity)  
\- Patterned SrTiO₃ substrate (from Step 5)  
\- High-purity O₂ gas (99.999%) 

**Experimental Instruments** :  
\- Pulsed Laser Deposition (PLD) system (KrF excimer laser, 248 nm)  
\- High-temperature furnace (up to 1000°C, oxygen atmosphere control)  
\- X-ray Diffractometer (XRD, PANalytical X'Pert PRO)  
\- Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM, FEI Talos F200X) 

**Experimental Procedure** : 

**Step 1: Substrate Preparation**  
1\. Clean the patterned SrTiO₃ substrate using a standard RCA cleaning process:  
\- Immerse the substrate in a 1:1:5 solution of NH₄OH:H₂O₂:H₂O at 75°C for 10 minutes.  
\- Rinse thoroughly with deionized water (DI water).  
\- Immerse the substrate in a 1:1:5 solution of HCl:H₂O₂:H₂O at 75°C for 10 minutes.  
\- Rinse again with DI water and dry with nitrogen gas. 

**Step 2: PLD System Setup**  
1\. Load the STO, LMO, and CeO₂ targets into the PLD system.  
2\. Set the substrate holder to the appropriate position, ensuring a target-substrate distance of 4-6 cm.  
3\. Preheat the substrate to 675°C under a base pressure of 1 × 10⁻⁶ Torr.  
4\. Introduce high-purity O₂ gas into the chamber to maintain a pressure of 200 mTorr during deposition. 

**Step 3: STO Buffer Layer Deposition**  
1\. Set the laser fluence to 2 J/cm² and the repetition rate to 5 Hz.  
2\. Deposit a 10 nm thick STO layer on the patterned SrTiO₃ substrate.  
3\. Monitor the deposition process using in-situ reflection high-energy electron diffraction (RHEED) to ensure epitaxial growth. 

**Step 4: LMO Buffer Layer Deposition**  
1\. Switch to the LMO target and maintain the same laser parameters.  
2\. Deposit a 10 nm thick LMO layer on top of the STO layer.  
3\. Use RHEED to confirm the epitaxial quality and lattice matching. 

**Step 5: CeO₂ Buffer Layer Deposition**  
1\. Switch to the CeO₂ target and maintain the same laser parameters.  
2\. Deposit a 10 nm thick CeO₂ layer on top of the LMO layer.  
3\. Use RHEED to confirm the epitaxial quality and lattice matching. 

**Step 6: Post-Deposition Annealing**  
1\. Perform in-situ annealing at 450°C for 30 minutes in 1 atm O₂ to optimize the oxygen content and crystallinity of the buffer layers.  
2\. Cool the substrate to room temperature at a controlled rate of 5°C/min to prevent thermal stress-induced cracking. 

**Step 7: Characterization**  
1\. Perform XRD analysis to confirm the crystallinity and lattice parameters of the buffer layers.  
2\. Use TEM to examine the interface quality and stepwise lattice transition between the layers.  
3\. Verify the thickness and uniformity of each buffer layer using cross-sectional TEM. 

**Experimental Precautions** :  
\- Ensure accurate control of the deposition rate and thickness of each buffer layer to achieve a stepwise lattice transition.  
\- Monitor the oxygen pressure and substrate temperature continuously to maintain optimal growth conditions.  
\- Use RHEED to confirm epitaxial growth and lattice matching during each deposition step.  
\- Minimize contamination by maintaining a clean chamber environment and handling the substrate with gloves. 

**Expected Outcomes** :  
\- A buffer layer stack (STO/LMO/CeO₂) with a total thickness of 30 nm, deposited epitaxially on the patterned SrTiO₃ substrate.  
\- Stepwise lattice transition confirmed by XRD and TEM, with minimal interfacial defects.  
\- Epitaxial quality verified by RHEED, showing sharp and streaky patterns.  
\- The buffer layer stack is ready for subsequent deposition of the REBCO superconducting film.
