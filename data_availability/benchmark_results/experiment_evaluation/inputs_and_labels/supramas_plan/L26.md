# Experimental Plan: REBCO current-flow-diversion tape and CORC® cable protocol

## Experimental Draft

### Stage 1 — Establish matched comparisons and qualify execution
The objective is to test whether interface-only current-flow diversion (CFD) increases normal-zone propagation velocity (NZPV) without processing-induced critical-current loss, and whether the effect survives bending, CORC® winding and thermal cycling. Compare regular and CFD straight tapes and cables from the same parent conductor lot, with bend coupons and separate structural witnesses. Three independent matched blocks are proposed; repeated pulses remain technical repeats.

Use supplied 4 mm REBCO conductors with existing buffers on 50 µm Hastelloy, a 1.6 µm superconducting layer and final 2.5 µm Ag. Required resources include qualified reel processing, cooled Ag sputtering, Cu plating, controlled-tension winding, structural and interface metrology, and protected synchronized transport instrumentation in pumped liquid nitrogen. Interface chemistry, processing parameters, cable geometry, trigger settings and energy limits require qualification before their respective operations.

### Stage 2 — Fabricate, release and establish transport baselines
Document incoming condition and pristine transport, qualify sacrificial coupons, then process the central 3.20 mm CFD zone while preserving symmetric edge contacts and underlying REBCO. Restore Ag without post-etch annealing. Verify interface resistance, coverage, continuity, structure and transport integrity before bending or winding. Assemble matched cables and qualify Cu-pad/indium-solder terminals. Establish structural comparisons and fitted 77 K self-field Ic and n-values using measured gauge lengths and current-transfer correction.

### Stage 3 — Map propagation, test retention and consolidate evidence
Perform protected local-trigger NZPV comparisons at common absolute currents and separately matched I/Ic. Follow initial cable characterization with the proposed single thermal cycle and repeat terminal, transport and propagation measurements. Retain failed and censored trials alongside accepted data.

Anticipated outputs are traceable paired comparisons, uncertainty-qualified retention estimates and threshold-defined propagation velocities, not guaranteed improvements. Proposed/P settings remain calibration-dependent; supplied performance values are reference targets only. Optional temperature and additional-cycle studies follow core completion, without introducing dopants, irradiation, replacement substrates or extra barrier layers.

### Experimental Task 2: Qualify materials, equipment, geometry and measurement settings

#### Specific Content

##### 2. Materials, equipment and release prerequisites
Use supplied REBCO coated conductors with existing buffers on 50 µm Hastelloy; superconducting thickness 1.6 µm, width 4 mm, final Ag thickness 2.5 µm. Obtain the qualified amorphous-interface chemistry, compatible mask/strip agents and selective etchant. The interface material and formation mechanism are unspecified: fabrication is blocked until coupon qualification establishes them.

Equipment: reel-to-reel masking/etching/rinsing line, cooled Ag sputtering system with calibrated thickness monitor, Cu electroplating station, controlled-tension CORC® winder, dimensional metrology, optical microscopy, SEM/EDS, XRD, interface-resistance fixture, optional cross-sectional TEM, pumped-LN₂ cryostat, calibrated four-wire PT100 sensors, pulsed-current source, low-noise four-probe instrumentation, synchronized multichannel DAQ, pogo-pin PCB, Hall probe and remotely positioned NdFeB magnet. Provide rated current leads, isolated voltage inputs, fast current interruption and energy dump.

Before release, freeze cable tape count, layer arrangement, pitch, winding handedness, tension, length and terminal overlap identically for R-C/F-C. Verify that 6.35 mm former OD and the actual stack produce the intended 6.9 mm bending geometry. Neither tape count nor pitch is supplied; do not infer them from cable Ic.

##### 3. Consolidated settings and consistency checks
Values marked **P** are proposed operating choices requiring calibration, not task-derived conditions.

| Quantity | Setting / gate |
|---|---|
| CFD width | 80% = 3.20 mm; **P** tolerance ±0.08 mm; symmetric exposed contact edges 0.40 mm each |
| Specific interface resistance, Ri | Task range 100–320 µΩ·cm² = 1.00–3.20×10⁻⁸ Ω·m²; working window 200±50 µΩ·cm² |
| Final Ag / terminal Cu | 2.5 µm / 10 µm; **P** thickness tolerance ±5%, verified on witnesses |
| Former / bending diameter | 6.35 / 6.9 mm; **P** diameter metrology tolerance ±0.1 mm |
| Temperature | 77 K primary; 67–77 K secondary; spatial uniformity ±0.05 K after calibrated sensor uncertainty is included |
| Ic criterion | E_c = 1 µV/cm = 10⁻⁴ V/m; V_c = E_c L for measured tap separation L |
| NZPV mapping | 16 cm cable window; nominal tap pitch 5.9 mm; 0.1 V threshold on defined local differential channels |
| Current pulses | **P** Ic start: 50 ms flat top, ≥1 s recovery, ±1% flat-top ripple; accept only after settling/heating checks |
| DAQ | **P** 1 MHz/channel synchronized acquisition; verify bandwidth, skew, voltage range and actual noise floor |
| Thermal cycling | **P** one 77→300→77 K cycle initially; ≤5 K/min during controlled warming/cooling where feasible |

**Required calculations.** A tape superconducting cross-section is 0.004×1.6×10⁻⁶ = 6.4×10⁻⁹ m². Thus Jc = 1.5×10¹¹ A/m² implies 960 A per tape, not the draft’s 120–150 A. Calculate measured Jc using this superconducting area; keep cable Ic separate from per-tape Jc. Select source capacity from actual specimens: a 500 A source cannot measure 581 A cable Ic or drive 1 kA quench tests, and cannot test the stated per-tape Jc target. Verify ≥1 kA pulse delivery and additional capacity if needed for Ic fitting, with adequate compliance and protection.

For 5.9 mm taps, the Ic criterion voltage is 0.59 µV; for 16 cm it is 16 µV. Verify noise and offset below the selected criterion; use separate longer Ic taps if necessary. Never substitute the 0.1 V quench threshold for the Ic criterion.

At 0.35 m/s, propagation over 5.9 mm takes 16.9 ms and over 16 cm about 0.46 s. Quench acquisition must cover sufficient adjacent crossings; the proposed 50 ms Ic pulse does not establish full-window NZPV. A longer protected quench pulse requires an independently qualified thermal-energy budget.

Known layer thickness totals 54.1 µm, excluding buffers/interface. A simple mid-plane bend estimate is |ε|≈t/D≈0.784% at 6.9 mm, not 2.2%. Measure the full stack and neutral-axis geometry. REBCO facing inward gives compression in a simple bend; verify actual helical strain, defining the allowable compression as ε > −1%.

- Proposed/P tolerances, pulse settings, acquisition settings and thermal-cycle choices require calibration and are distinct from supplied conditions and release gates. **Source:** B007, B008.

### Experimental Task 3: Qualify and fabricate CFD tapes, perform bend tests and assemble matched cables

#### Specific Content

##### 4. Sequential fabrication and hand-offs
1. **Incoming inspection → baseline transport.** Photograph both surfaces and edges; map thickness/width, defects and reel position. Measure 77 K self-field Ic and n at multiple longitudinal positions. Acquire baseline REBCO diffraction on witnesses. Quarantine cracked, delaminated or electrically discontinuous stock.
2. **Coupon qualification → approved reel recipe.** On sacrificial matched coupons, qualify nonabrasive cleaning, selective removal of existing Ag where needed, mask compatibility, amorphous-interface formation and subsequent Ag restoration. Do not use an unqualified HF recipe, ultrasonic exposure or thermal treatment on production tape. Establish etch endpoint, undercut limits, reel speed, rinsing, drying, mask removal and maximum substrate temperature experimentally. Record chemistry, concentrations, exposure times and deposition settings in the approved run sheet.
3. **Reel processing → interface QA.** Mask the two low-Ri edge strips. Apply the qualified etch/interface-forming sequence only to the central 3.20 mm zone, preserving the REBCO underneath. Rinse, dry and unmask using qualified agents. Confirm clean continuous edge-contact regions. Sputter Ag to a final total 2.5 µm using calibrated rate and actual exposure time; no post-etch anneal. Maintain reel tension below the qualified damage limit. Retain beginning/middle/end witnesses.
4. **Interface QA → released tape.** Inspect boundary continuity and widths optically/SEM. Determine Ri using patterned four-terminal transfer structures and a validated current-transfer model including lateral Ag spreading; sheet resistance alone is insufficient. Check both high-Ri and low-Ri zones. Cross-sectional microscopy verifies layer ordering and continuity; electron diffraction on selected witnesses assesses amorphous character. SEM/EDS cannot establish absence of lattice distortion.
5. **Released tape → bend test/winding.** Re-measure straight-tape Ic/n. For R-B/F-B, perform one controlled bend at 6.9 mm with matched compressive orientation and measure Ic while held at the specified geometry. Record curvature and any subsequent straightening separately. For cables, wind released tapes on the copper former with frozen geometry and no inter-layer insulation; log tension and visible defects by layer.
6. **Wound cable → terminal QA.** Electrodeposit 10 µm Cu pads at tape terminal regions before indium soldering to copper terminals. Qualify plating compatibility and minimum effective solder temperature/dwell on witnesses; record actual thermal history. Measure terminal specific contact resistance using measured overlap area and a geometry-aware model; target <1 µΩ·cm². Reject voided, unstable or degraded joints before high-current testing.

#### Related Knowledge

- The sequential hand-offs require incoming inspection and coupon-qualified cleaning, etching, interface formation and Ag restoration before production processing. **Source:** B013.
- Interface release combines boundary inspection, geometry-aware four-terminal resistance measurements and structural witnesses; sheet resistance and SEM/EDS alone cannot establish all required interface or lattice properties. **Source:** B013.
- Released tapes undergo transport remeasurement before matched bend testing or winding; terminal plating and soldering require witness qualification and contact-resistance acceptance before high-current testing. **Source:** B013.

### Experimental Task 4: Verify structural preservation and measure fitted self-field Ic and n

#### Specific Content

##### 5. Characterization and transport sequence
**Structural gate.** Compare pre/post REBCO θ–2θ scans and texture using identical instrument alignment. Proposed reflections: REBCO (005) ω rocking curve for out-of-plane mosaic and an accessible indexed off-axis REBCO reflection, such as (103), for φ scans. Confirm peak assignment and separation from buffers before use. Report Δω and Δφ separately: ω width is not in-plane alignment. Treat the supplied <5° texture target as requiring clarification; meanwhile require no resolved processing-induced broadening or lattice change relative to matched witnesses. Orthorhombicity may need higher-resolution diffraction than laboratory scans provide.

**Ic.** Mount four-probe taps outside terminal/current-transfer regions; record exact gauge lengths. Cool and map PT100 temperatures until stable and spatially within ±0.05 K. Withdraw the NdFeB magnet for self-field measurements. Begin well below expected Ic, increase current coarsely, then refine near the criterion. Record current and voltage waveforms, remove calibrated inductive/offset contributions and subtract independently assessed current-transfer voltage. Fit E = E_c(I/Ic)^n over a preregistered noise- and heating-free range; do not define Ic by averaging bracketing pulses. Report fit uncertainty and raw/corrected curves. Confirm pulse-duration independence before accepting Ic.

#### Related Knowledge

- Matched diffraction alignment and separately reported rocking-curve and azimuthal widths prevent conflating out-of-plane mosaic with in-plane alignment; the supplied texture target remains unresolved. **Source:** B014.
- Four-probe transport requires stable mapped temperature, exact gauge lengths, self-field conditions and independently assessed current-transfer-voltage subtraction. **Source:** B015.
- Ic and n are obtained from a preregistered noise- and heating-free fit range, with uncertainty, raw/corrected curves and pulse-duration independence documented. **Source:** B015.

### Experimental Task 5: Map protected NZPV and repeat cable characterization after thermal cycling

#### Specific Content

**NZPV.** Install the pogo-pin PCB over the 16 cm cable window; survey actual longitudinal tap coordinates, channel polarities and differential-pair geometry. Use analogous 5.9 mm mapping on straight tapes where practical. Calibrate trigger-field profile and repeatable magnet position with a Hall probe; field amplitude and dwell are unresolved until qualification. Trigger locally at the center under constant current. Keep cooling, trigger geometry and applied current identical within comparisons. Test common absolute-current points and separately matched I/Ic points; include 1 kA only after safe lower-current trials.

Acquire all channels continuously before magnet actuation. Determine each local channel’s 0.1 V crossing relative to its pre-trigger baseline; fit position versus arrival time separately in each direction using at least three valid channels. Exclude the directly magnet-affected region. Report threshold-defined velocity and sensitivity to threshold/noise. Pogo contact instability, current-transfer signals or dump-induced voltages invalidate affected crossings. If interruption occurs before enough crossings, report a censored test, not a velocity.

**Thermal cycle and post-test hand-off.** After initial cable characterization, execute the proposed single thermal cycle using recorded temperature ramps and equilibrium endpoints, dry purge during warming and no forced mechanical manipulation. Repeat terminal resistance, 77 K Ic/n and protected NZPV. Inspect quench sites and terminals optically after every event; repeat transport checks before reuse.

#### Related Knowledge

- Surveyed tap geometry and qualified magnet positioning support matched propagation comparisons at common absolute current and separately matched I/Ic; trigger amplitude and dwell remain qualification-dependent. **Source:** B016.
- Threshold-defined velocities require valid local crossings in each direction outside the directly triggered region; invalid signals are excluded and insufficient crossings produce censored tests. **Source:** B017.
- The proposed initial thermal cycle follows cable characterization and is followed by terminal resistance, Ic/n and protected NZPV remeasurement, with event inspection and transport checks before reuse. **Source:** B018.

### Experimental Task 6: Apply release gates, diagnose failures and archive paired comparisons

#### Specific Content

##### 6. Quality gates, diagnosis and controlled feedback
Release fabrication only when coverage, Ri, Ag thickness, interface continuity and transport integrity pass. Define “no Ic degradation” before testing as paired retention within validated measurement uncertainty, rather than assuming exact equality. Supplied references are 4–4.5× NZPV enhancement, 0.35 m/s at 1 kA, cable Ic 581→540 A, n≈22 and bend retention 75%/68%; these are comparison targets, not automatic acceptance results. The supplied thermal retention corresponds to 92.9%.

Low Ic after processing: isolate etch damage, oxygen/thermal exposure, edge contamination or measurement artifacts on witnesses; quarantine affected lengths. Wrong Ri: recalibrate interface formation and transfer-model geometry, then process fresh coupons. Winding loss: inspect tension, compressive strain and delamination; do not change only one cable arm. High terminal resistance: inspect pad continuity, solder consumption and voids; requalify joints before retesting. No local quench: verify field position and local suppression before changing trigger conditions; never automatically raise current or magnet dwell. No 0.1 V crossing before safe cutoff: retain raw traces and flag threshold feasibility as unresolved.

Store sample lineage, run sheets, geometry, calibration files, microscopy/XRD data, temperatures, synchronized waveforms, protection trips, fit code/settings, uncertainties and all failed trials. Compare paired CFD/regular ratios within tape and cable architectures; assess architecture interaction without assuming multiplicative gains. Freeze conditions before each matched block. Changes require documented cause, coupon validation and new matched controls; do not tune only successful specimens.

##### 7. Safety and optional extensions
Use wet-chemistry SOPs, compatible containment and approved waste streams; HF requires dedicated authorization and emergency arrangements if the qualified chemistry uses it. Provide cryogen ventilation, oxygen-deficiency monitoring, pressure relief, frost protection and dry purging. Guard magnet motion against pinch/projectile hazards. Interlock high-current operation against overvoltage, excess energy and measured hot-spot limits qualified on sacrificial specimens; voltage mapping is not the sole protection mechanism.

Optional, after core completion: additional matched tests at 67–77 K and additional logged thermal cycles. They must retain the four core comparison arms. High-field application claims require separate field-capable equipment and are outside this self-field/local-trigger protocol.

#### Related Knowledge

- No-degradation acceptance is defined by paired retention within validated uncertainty; supplied enhancement, velocity, current, n-value and retention figures are references rather than measured results. **Source:** B019.
- Failures require documented diagnosis, quarantine or requalification as appropriate; unsuccessful triggering or missing threshold crossings do not justify automatic increases in current or magnet dwell. **Source:** B020.
- Complete lineage, calibration, waveform, uncertainty and failed-trial records support paired architecture-specific comparisons; changes require coupon validation and new matched controls. **Source:** B021.
- Chemical, cryogenic, magnetic and high-current safeguards are prerequisites, while additional temperatures and cycles are optional after core completion and high-field claims remain outside scope. **Source:** B022, B023.
