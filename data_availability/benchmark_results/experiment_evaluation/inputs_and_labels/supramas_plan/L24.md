# Experimental Plan: YBCO on STO–CeO₂ nanocomposite buffers

## Experimental Draft

### Objective and comparison design
Reconstruct a controlled comparison of 300-nm YBCO films on PLD STO–CeO₂ nanocomposite buffers on LAO, testing the proposed relationship between buffer morphology, interfacial BaCeO₃, threading screw dislocations and perpendicular-field Jc. Texture and superconducting integrity remain independent assessment requirements; supplied structural and transport values are reference targets, not measured outcomes.

Compare 0, 30 and 50 vol% CeO₂ at buffer deposition temperatures of 800 and 850 °C, with YBCO growth held at 800 °C. Confirm the assignment of the temperature comparison to buffer growth before release. The proposed design uses three independently deposited films per cell, with randomized replicate blocks, buffer-only witnesses and linked transport/microscopy specimens. The 0% cells are mandatory controls; the supplied 3 vol% BaSnO₃ reference remains contextual unless qualified physical references are available.

### Resources and workflow
Use qualified composite and stoichiometric YBCO targets, LAO substrates, oxygen and validated processing/contact materials. Required capabilities include interlocked PLD, calibrated temperature/pressure/thickness measurements, XRD, AFM, bridge fabrication, four-probe transport through 9 T, and FIB/TEM/STEM-EDX characterization.

Release prerequisites and proposed settings before comparative fabrication. Deposit composite buffers—not separate STO/CeO₂ layers—then YBCO and a common qualified oxygenation cycle. Perform nondestructive mapping, pattern and measure bridges, and only then prepare destructive cross-section and plan-view specimens from mapped parent material.

### Constraints and anticipated outputs
Resolve the laser-wavelength discrepancy, deposition-rate calibration, oxygenation compatibility and ampere-level transport capability. Preserve proposed settings, optional steps and qualification gates explicitly. Expected outputs are traceable process records, texture/morphology maps, qualified phase and defect measurements, normalized Jc curves and comparison summaries retaining failures, uncertainty and valid low-performing films.

### Experimental Task 1: Qualify resources, targets and the frozen comparison matrix

#### Specific Content

##### 1. Objective, identifiers and comparisons

**Status:** T = task condition; D = consistent draft selection; P = proposed choice requiring calibration. All unmarked operational choices below are P. Freeze them before comparative fabrication.

Prepare three independently deposited films per cell, `Cxx-Tyyy-R01…03` (18 films). Use proposed LAO(001), 5 × 5 mm, from one lot; randomize runs within replicate blocks. Add buffer-only witnesses `-W`; link bridges `-B01` and cross-section/plan-view specimens `-XS01/-PV01` to parent IDs. Record chamber history and target changes.

##### 2. Materials, equipment and prerequisite release
Materials: stoichiometric YBCO target; STO/CeO₂ composite targets or powders; LAO; high-purity O₂; cleaning solvents; lithography supplies; qualified Au contacts, wiring and mounts. Do not introduce unvalidated CuO excess.

Equipment: interlocked PLD with target rotation/rastering, calibrated thermometry and pressure gauge; target-processing equipment if needed; XRD, AFM and thickness metrology; FIB; TEM/HR-TEM/STEM-EDX with SAED/two-beam capability; lithography/ion milling; PPMS-compatible 9 T transport system with adequate external current source and low-noise voltmeter if necessary.

**Hold before fabrication:** resolve task-specified KrF 243 nm against actual laser certification and the draft's 248 nm. Standard KrF emission is approximately 248 nm; authorize and record any departure explicitly. Verify 850 °C heating, oxygenation pressure compatibility, 65–77 K/9 T sample-space capability and ampere-level current delivery. RHEED is optional and requires pressure compatibility; oscillations are not a rough-buffer acceptance gate.

##### 4. Sequential fabrication and hand-offs

###### A. Targets → PLD inventory
Calculate powder masses from volume fraction f using `mCe/mSTO = fρCe/[(1−f)ρSTO]`, with verified phase densities. Record densities, masses and uncertainty; reject weight-percent or fixed-molar-ratio substitutions.

Prefer qualified targets. An in-house pilot route may use ethanol milling with zirconia media, 10:1 media/powder, 200 rpm, 12 h; drying at 80 °C for 6 h; qualified PVA addition/burnout; pressing up to 400 MPa and 200 MPa CIP; oxygen sintering at 950 °C for 12 h, heating/cooling at 5/2 °C/min. These are unvalidated starting choices: qualify binder removal, tooling limits, chemical stability and densification before matrix use.

XRD must show intended STO and CeO₂ phases, not a single “phase-pure composite.” Inspect homogeneity, contamination, density, cracking and stable ablation. Proposed relative-density goal: ≥95%; the schedule does not guarantee it. Reject friable/altered targets. Transfer signed qualification records to PLD staff.

### Experimental Task 2: Deposit buffer–YBCO stacks and perform nondestructive structural mapping

#### Specific Content

###### B. LAO → buffer → YBCO
1. Assign IDs and photograph substrates. Clean in acetone, IPA and DI water, proposed 10 min each; N₂ dry. If required, qualify a common 600 °C, 1 h oxygen pre-clean.
2. Mount with qualified thermal attachment. Calibrate surface temperature against heater indication.
3. Pump down, establish specified O₂, pre-ablate and deposit the assigned **composite** buffer. Log temperature, pressure, energy and pulses. Do not replace it with discrete layers.
4. Deposit buffer-only witnesses alongside where possible. Preserve them for AFM/TEM; retain main stacks in vacuum without solvent cleaning or separate buffer annealing.
5. Equilibrate every main stack at 800 °C for the same calibrated interval; deposit 300-nm YBCO from one qualified target. Apply the common oxygenation cycle. If 1 atm is unavailable, qualify a compatible common cycle before comparative runs.
6. Cool; inspect cracking/particulates; map thickness, XRD and AFM. Transfer samples, maps and logs to characterization/patterning staff. Retain failure records.

##### 5. Structure and defect measurements
Acquire XRD θ–2θ, rocking curves and in-plane orientation scans. Report c-axis texture, a-axis grains, secondary phases and detection limits. Measure buffer AFM roughness and lateral spacing; approximately 20-nm wall spacing is an objective, not an atomic-flatness gate.

The draft's lattice constants give STO/YBCO mismatch approximately 2.23% along a and 0.39% along b, not uniformly <1%. Measure orientation, strain and relaxation; do not assign an averaged composite lattice constant or infer coherency from target XRD. With Cu Kα λ = 1.5406 Å, STO(002) and YBCO(005) are approximately 46.5° and 38.5° in 2θ. Verify wavelength/indexing. YBCO d005 ≈0.234 nm does not demonstrate <0.2-nm microscope resolution.

#### Related Knowledge

- The fabrication hand-off preserves a composite buffer architecture, common YBCO growth conditions and oxygenation, and buffer-only witnesses. Main stacks remain in vacuum without separate buffer annealing or solvent cleaning. **Source:** B015.
- XRD and AFM assess texture, secondary phases, roughness and lateral spacing. The wall-spacing objective is not an atomic-flatness acceptance requirement. **Source:** B016.
- Orientation, strain and relaxation require measurement rather than inference from an averaged composite lattice constant or target diffraction. Wavelength and indexing checks also prevent diffraction spacings from being misrepresented as microscope-resolution evidence. **Source:** B017.

### Experimental Task 3: Prepare post-transport microscopy specimens and quantify interface phases and defects

#### Specific Content

**Transport precedes destructive FIB.** Reserve mapped unpatterned regions, then transfer tested parent material and locations to FIB staff. Prepare both cross-section and plan-view lift-outs. Starting preparation: electron-beam protection followed by approximately 1 µm Pt, 30 kV coarse milling with decreasing current, ≤5 kV final polishing; optional low-energy Ar cleanup requires qualification. Record thickness and damage.

At proposed 200 kV, establish a non-damaging dose window. Map Ce, Ba, Sr, Ti, Y and Cu; combine EDX with SAED/lattice imaging to identify CeO₂ walls and BaCeO₃. Ba:Ce alone is insufficient. Measure ≥50 particles per cell where available. Examine multiple cross-sections, including approximately 1-µm-wide regions, for texture and full-thickness threading.

Count defects in ≥10 separated plan-view fields totaling ≥10 µm² per film where feasible; report actual counts/area and uncertainty. Use multiple indexed two-beam g vectors, invisibility tests and line directions to distinguish screw, edge and mixed character.

Density conversion: 20–150 µm⁻² = 2 × 10⁹–1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻². For confirmed threading pins, `Bφ = nΦ0`, Φ0 = 2.07 × 10⁻¹⁵ Wb, gives 0.041–0.311 T. Do not substitute nanoparticle density for threading-pin density.

#### Related Knowledge

- Destructive FIB preparation follows transport, using reserved mapped regions and parent-linked locations. Preparation damage and specimen thickness are recorded, and optional cleanup remains subject to qualification. **Source:** B018.
- Elemental mapping must be combined with diffraction or lattice imaging to identify CeO₂ walls and BaCeO₃; Ba:Ce composition alone is insufficient. Dose qualification and multiple cross-sections support interpretable interface and threading observations. **Source:** B019.
- Defect counts require actual sampled areas, uncertainty and indexed diffraction-contrast tests to distinguish screw, edge and mixed character. Matching-field conversion applies to confirmed threading pins, not nanoparticle counts. **Source:** B020, B021.

### Experimental Task 4: Pattern qualified bridges and measure perpendicular-field critical current before FIB

#### Specific Content

##### 6. Patterning and transport
Qualify resist/exposure/development and low-damage Ar milling on sacrificial material. Pattern 100-µm bridges with separate current pads and internal voltage taps; verify isolation, edges, width, thickness and tap spacing. Use validated Au contacts without an unqualified deoxygenating Ar anneal.

Nominal cross-section: `100 µm × 300 nm = 3 × 10⁻⁷ cm²`. The supplied 3 and 1 MA/cm² targets therefore require **0.9 and 0.3 A**; self-field current may be higher. Qualify source, feedthrough, puck, wiring and contact heating; do not presume the PPMS resistivity option suffices. At 0.9 A, a 1 Ω current contact dissipates 0.81 W. Set current/thermal abort limits from qualification.

Hand off measured geometry, contact checks and current ratings. Measure low-current R(T) through the transition. Mount **B parallel to c**, normal to film, with in-plane current.

Zero-field cool for each temperature series; stabilize for ≥5 min within ±0.1 K. Ramp fields upward, including 1 and 5 T; acquire three bidirectional I–V sweeps at each settled point. Calibrate pulse duration, duty cycle and acquisition settling; monitor heating and offsets. A slow continuous ramp does not guarantee thermal safety.

Use `Vc = (10 µV/cm)L`: at L = 1.00 mm, **Vc = 1.00 µV**. Qualify noise below proposed 0.1 µV. Subtract thermoelectric offsets, interpolate Ic at Vc, and calculate `Jc = Ic/(wt)` from measured dimensions. If compliance is reached first, report a lower bound.

#### Related Knowledge

- Bridge patterning and contacts require sacrificial-material qualification and measured geometry. The supplied current-density targets imply ampere-level delivery requirements, so current-path heating and abort limits cannot be assumed from the transport instrument alone. **Source:** B022, B023.
- Low-current transition measurements and field alignment establish the transport configuration. Temperature stabilization, repeated bidirectional sweeps and qualified pulse/acquisition timing address heating and offset risks. **Source:** B024, B025.
- The electric-field criterion is converted to a voltage using measured tap separation, and Jc is normalized by measured width and thickness. Offset correction, noise qualification and explicit compliance-limited lower bounds preserve interpretable transport records. **Source:** B026.

### Experimental Task 5: Apply failure gates, consolidate records and compare structure with transport

#### Specific Content

##### 7. Gates, diagnosis, records and feedback
Abort for unsafe operation, contact deterioration, overheating, irreversible I–V changes or damage. Heating: improve contacts/reduce duty cycle and requalify. Poor R(T): check oxygenation and control films. Texture loss/cracks: check thermometry, mounting, plume and thickness. Missing walls/particles: check composition transfer/interface chemistry. Microscopy artifacts: reprepare or lower dose before counting.

Replace technical failures only with documented causes under frozen conditions. Valid low Jc or sparse defects remain results, not exclusions. Test corrections in separate calibration batches; do not optimize individual matrix cells mid-comparison.

Archive IDs, raw process/calibration logs, geometry, contact ratings, diffraction/images, EDX spectra, counting masks, R(T), all I–V curves and dispositions. Separate independent-film dispersion from technical-repeat variability; propagate geometry uncertainty. Compare composition, temperature and interaction effects against microscopy and Jc at 65/77 K, 1 T. Field exponents alone cannot establish exclusive screw-dislocation pinning or unchanged superconducting volume.

Use laser/beam/high-voltage interlocks, oxygen-clean gas handling, press/furnace limits, solvent ventilation, heavy-metal waste containment and magnet exclusion/quench procedures.

**Optional extension:** angular transport after core completion may test c-axis correlated pinning; it cannot replace perpendicular-field controls or microscopy.

#### Related Knowledge

- Unsafe operation, heating, deteriorating contacts and irreversible changes trigger aborts. Corrections belong in separate calibration batches; only documented technical failures may be replaced, while valid low Jc or sparse defects remain results. **Source:** B027, B028.
- Traceable raw records and propagated geometry uncertainty support comparison of composition, buffer temperature and their interaction. Independent-film dispersion must remain separate from technical-repeat variability, and field exponents alone cannot establish an exclusive pinning mechanism. **Source:** B029.
- The supplied safety controls apply throughout execution. Angular transport is an optional post-core extension and cannot substitute for perpendicular-field controls or microscopy. **Source:** B030, B031.

## Refined Experimental Plan

### 1. Release the comparison and resources
Use the six composition–temperature cells: 0, 30 and 50 vol% CeO₂ buffers deposited at 800 or 850 °C, followed by 300-nm YBCO at 800 °C. Confirm the buffer-temperature assignment before release. Retain 0% controls. The proposed design comprises three independent films per cell, randomized within replicate blocks, with linked buffer witnesses, bridges and microscopy specimens. Freeze all proposed choices before comparative fabrication; unmarked operational choices remain P. Supplied structural and Jc values are targets only. The 3 vol% BaSnO₃ reference is contextual unless qualified physical specimens are measured identically with fabrication differences documented.

Resolve the task-specified 243-nm laser wavelength against actual certification and the approximately 248-nm draft value; explicitly authorize departures. Verify temperature, oxygenation, sample-space and current-delivery capabilities. Apply laser/beam/high-voltage interlocks, oxygen-clean handling, press/furnace limits, solvent ventilation, heavy-metal containment and magnet exclusion/quench procedures throughout.

Prefer qualified targets and a stoichiometric YBCO target without unvalidated CuO excess. Calculate composite powder masses from volume fractions and verified densities. Any in-house target route remains a pilot until binder removal, tooling, chemical stability, densification and stable ablation are qualified; confirm intended STO and CeO₂ phases rather than claiming a single phase-pure composite.

### 2. Fabricate and map the stacks
Calibrate deposition rate for every target/composition and verify thickness independently; do not use the assumed pulse rate as established. Retain 10 Hz and 200 mTorr O₂ for both deposition stages. Proposed fluence, separation, base pressure, pre-ablation, tolerances and 30-nm buffer thickness require release under the consolidated parameter table.

Identify, photograph, clean and mount LAO using qualified procedures and calibrated surface thermometry. Deposit the assigned composite buffer, not discrete STO/CeO₂ layers. Preserve buffer-only witnesses; keep main stacks in vacuum without solvent cleaning or separate buffer annealing. Equilibrate every main stack at 800 °C for the same calibrated interval, deposit YBCO from one qualified target and apply the common qualified oxygenation cycle. The proposed cycle is cooling to 450 °C at 10 °C/min, holding 30 min at 1 atm O₂, then cooling at 5 °C/min; qualify a compatible common alternative before matrix runs if necessary.

Inspect cracks and particulates, map thickness, XRD texture/orientation and AFM morphology, and retain failures. Assess strain and relaxation without assuming composite coherency. Wall spacing is an objective, not an atomic-flatness gate; optional RHEED is not required for rough-buffer acceptance.

### 3. Complete transport before destructive microscopy
Qualify lithography, low-damage milling and Au contacts on sacrificial material. Pattern 100-µm bridges and measure actual width, thickness and tap spacing. Qualify the full current path, contact heating, noise and abort limits: nominal target currents are 0.9 and 0.3 A, not measured outcomes. Obtain low-current R(T), then measure with field normal to the film, parallel to c, and in-plane current.

Use the proposed 65, 70 and 77 K grid and 0–9 T upward field sequence in 0.5 T steps, including 1 and 5 T. Zero-field cool each temperature series, stabilize for at least 5 min within the proposed ±0.1 K tolerance, and acquire three bidirectional sweeps per settled point with qualified timing. Correct offsets; calculate the threshold from 10 µV/cm and measured tap separation, then normalize Ic by measured cross-section. Report compliance-limited lower bounds and geometry uncertainty.

Only after transport, prepare mapped cross-section and plan-view FIB specimens. Record thickness and damage, qualify microscopy dose, and combine STEM-EDX with diffraction/lattice imaging for phase identification. Follow source sampling provisions for particles, cross-sections and separated plan-view fields; report actual counts, areas and uncertainty. Establish defect character with indexed two-beam tests and line directions. Calculate matching field only from confirmed threading-pin density.

### 4. Close out without selective optimization
Abort unsafe or damaging operation and document dispositions. Requalify corrections in separate calibration batches; replace only documented technical failures under frozen conditions. Retain valid low Jc and sparse defects. Archive process logs, geometry, contacts, raw transport and microscopy records. Compare composition, temperature and interaction effects, separating independent-film dispersion from technical repeats. Do not infer exclusive screw-dislocation pinning from field exponents alone. Optional angular transport follows core completion and does not replace controls or microscopy.
