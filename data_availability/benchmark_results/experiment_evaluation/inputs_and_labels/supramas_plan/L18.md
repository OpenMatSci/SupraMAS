# Experimental Plan: REBCO face-to-face double-stack protocol

## Experimental Draft

### Stage I — Release materials and fabricate constituent tapes
Reconstruct the frozen protocol as a comparison of individual YBCO, EuBCO containing nominal 3.5 mol% BHO, and their electrically parallel face-to-face double stack (FFDS). Use three position-traceable matched pairs and adjacent witnesses. The objective is to test current summation, minimum critical current, and angular variation at 65 K and 1.5 T; supplied performance values remain reference targets, not measured outcomes.

Retain the Hastelloy C-276/IBAD-MgO/LMO/REBCO architecture. Before fabrication, resolve the BHO molar denominator, full template stack, film-thickness recipe, and electrically connected solderable metallization. Use certified targets, qualified templates, reel-to-reel PLD, calibrated oxygenation, and measured deposition rates. Proposed starting settings require qualification rather than automatic adoption.

### Stage II — Qualify constituents and assemble registered stacks
Use diffraction, thickness metrology, and witness microscopy to establish texture, continuity, and identifiable BHO inclusions/nanorods inside EuBCO. Complete individual angular transport measurements before assembly and preserve the measured gauge regions. Required resources include XRD, SEM/EDS, FIB/TEM, and a tension-controlled solder laminator. Join qualified conductive faces with Sn63Pb37 at the 200 °C task target and qualify the final 2–4 µm bond line, wetting, thermal exposure, alignment, and electrical transfer.

### Stage III — Measure stacks and consolidate interpretation
Use protected four-probe transport, a stable cryostat, magnet, rotation stage, and synchronized acquisition for identical constituent and stack measurements. Verify current capacity, noise, transfer length, and safety interlocks. Analyze current summation and angular statistics with propagated uncertainty, and constrain interface modeling using constituent curves and qualified transfer information. Anticipated outputs are traceable raw curves, structural and bond maps, documented fits or bounds, comparison statistics, and retained failure records. Optional controls remain separate from the core comparison.

## Detailed Experimental Tasks

### Experimental Task 1: Stage I — Qualify resources, targets, templates, and fabrication release

#### Specific Content

##### 1. Objective, matrix and release prerequisites
Test whether complementary YBCO and EuBCO+3.5 mol% BHO angular critical-current profiles add in an electrically parallel FFDS conductor at 65 K, 1.5 T, increasing minimum current and reducing angular variation. BHO must form inclusions/nanorods **inside EuBCO**, not a separate layer. Supplied current-summation, interface-resistivity and anisotropy values are comparison targets, not promised results.

Use the following proposed matrix, with three matched pairs from position-traceable reels:

| Identifier | Specimen and role |
|---|---|
| P01–P03-Y | Undoped YBCO; measure before assembly |
| P01–P03-E | EuBCO+3.5 mol% BHO; measure before assembly |
| P01–P03-F | FFDS assembled from the corresponding measured Y and E coupons |
| Pxx-Y-W, E-W, F-W | Adjacent witnesses for destructive microscopy, thermal-exposure checks and interface qualification |

Both tapes retain Hastelloy C-276/IBAD-MgO/LMO/REBCO architecture. Preserve measured coupon regions during assembly. If that is impractical, qualify longitudinal uniformity and document the uncertainty introduced by adjacent substitutes.

Before releasing fabrication, resolve the BHO molar denominator, full template stack, film thickness recipe, solderable surface metallization and its thickness. A qualified conductive cap may be necessary for soldering; it must contact REBCO electrically, be identical across relevant controls, and must not replace the EuBCO nanocomposite. Do not solder directly onto an unqualified oxide surface.

##### 2. Materials, equipment and consolidated settings
Use composition-certified YBCO and EuBCO/BHO composite PLD targets, qualified buffered C-276 reels, Sn63Pb37 solder, compatible flux if required, lint-free wipes, approved cleaning solvent, contact metallization and high-current terminals. Record lot certificates and all added conductive layers. Do not introduce CuO excess or substitute SrTiO3 substrates.

Equipment: reel-to-reel PLD with calibrated heater, oxygen control and laser fluence; qualified template source or IBAD/cap deposition line; tension-controlled solder laminator; thickness metrology; XRD pole-figure/φ-scan apparatus; SEM/EDS, FIB and TEM; cryostat capable of stable 65 K; 1.5 T magnet and rotation stage; protected current supply, nanovoltmeter and synchronized acquisition. Instrument ranges and noise require verification, not assumption.

Except for task targets explicitly identified below, settings are **proposed starting choices requiring calibration**.

| Operation | Setting, units and tolerance |
|---|---|
| Template texture, task target | LMO 200 φ-scan FWHM approximately 6–7° |
| Coupon geometry | Width 4 mm; length 30 mm; nominal REBCO thickness 1 µm per tape; measure actual values |
| PLD | Y: 750 ± 5 °C; E: 780 ± 5 °C; O₂: 200 ± 10 mTorr = 26.7 ± 1.3 Pa |
| Laser starting setup | KrF 248 nm; 2.5 ± 0.2 J/cm²; 8 Hz; target distance 50 mm; qualify footprint/uniformity |
| Oxygenation starting recipe | Y: 450 °C, 30 min, oxygen near 1 atm, cooling 10 °C/min; E: 500 °C, 60 min, oxygen near 1 atm, cooling 5 °C/min; temperature tolerance ±5 °C |
| Soldering, task targets | 200 °C; final bond line 2–4 µm |
| Soldering starting controls | 200 ± 5 °C; pressure 0.5 ± 0.1 MPa; speed 5 ± 1 cm/min; dwell 30 ± 5 s; lateral offset ≤0.1 mm |
| Transport | 65.0 ± 0.1 K; 1.50 ± 0.01 T; angular accuracy ±0.5°; −40° to 220°, 5° grid |
| Electrical criterion | 1 µV/cm; nominal voltage separation 5 mm; current-terminal separation 20 mm |

Verify thermal-contact geometry, terminal ratings and safe tape tension on witnesses. Do not adopt the draft's 10 N tension without mechanical qualification.

##### 3. Sequential fabrication and hand-offs

###### A. Targets and templates → PLD operator
1. Prefer qualified targets. If manufacturing in-house, resolve the 3.5 mol% basis before weighing; record masses, mixing, milling contamination, binder burnout, pressing and oxygen sintering. The draft's 300 rpm/4 h milling and 950 °C/12 h sintering are trial candidates only; qualify furnace behavior, target density, cracking and ablation before core growth.
2. Verify EuBCO/BHO composition using suitable bulk chemistry and XRD/SEM-EDS. BHO is an intended secondary phase: do not apply a single-phase EuBCO purity gate that excludes it. Target powder morphology does not establish film nanorods.
3. Receive qualified IBAD-MgO/LMO-buffered C-276, or fabricate using the laboratory's qualified template process. Do not apply bare-metal polishing/acid etching to completed buffers. Confirm cap continuity, contamination and longitudinal texture on witnesses.
4. Hand off target certificates, template maps, identifiers and release signatures. Hold damaged or mistextured templates for investigation; do not average rejected regions into acceptable material.

#### Related Knowledge

- The matched constituent/stack matrix and position-traceable witnesses establish the comparison basis. Preserving measured regions, or documenting qualified adjacent substitutions, retains the linkage between constituent and stack measurements. **Source:** B000, B001, B002, B003.
- Fabrication release depends on resolving composition, template, thickness, and solderable-metallization prerequisites. Qualified conductive caps must electrically contact REBCO without replacing the EuBCO nanocomposite. **Source:** B004, B005.
- Target chemistry and template qualification are distinct from demonstrating film nanorods. The source distinguishes task targets from proposed starting settings and requires calibration, witness checks, and traceable hand-offs. **Source:** B006, B007, B008, B009, B011.

### Experimental Task 2: Stage I — Deposit, oxygenate, and prepare traceable constituent coupons

#### Specific Content

##### B. PLD → structural/electrical characterization
1. Clean qualified capped templates using a validated non-damaging procedure. Load reels, calibrate substrate temperature, pressure and laser footprint, and pre-ablate targets with the substrate shielded; 500 shots at 5 Hz is a proposed starting clean.
2. Deposit separate YBCO and EuBCO+BHO reels using the table. Calibrate deposition rate on witnesses; derive reel residence time from measured rate and exposed length. Do not infer thickness from elapsed chamber time alone.
3. For nominal rates 0.5 nm/s (Y) and 0.4 nm/s (E), 1 µm requires 2000 s and 2500 s exposure respectively. A 45 min exposure gives 1.35 µm or 1.08 µm, not 150 nm. Adjust speed using measured rate, not these nominal estimates.
4. Oxygenate and cool using qualified composition-specific recipes. Record actual temperature/oxygen traces. Measure thickness at distributed reel locations, then cut coupons and adjacent witnesses with edge-damage controls. Apply qualified conductive caps/contact preparations consistently.
5. Transfer coupons for texture, microscopy and preassembly transport. Release only after confirming intact films, superconductivity comfortably below the intended operating temperature, and suitable epitaxy. Tc is diagnostic; the draft's >90 K/>85 K values are not task-mandated acceptance limits.

#### Related Knowledge

- Measured deposition rate and exposed reel length determine residence time; elapsed chamber time alone does not establish film thickness. Distributed thickness measurements support subsequent dimensional normalization. **Source:** B012, B008.
- Composition-specific oxygenation, actual process traces, edge-damage controls, and consistent conductive contact preparation support the characterization hand-off. Film integrity, suitable epitaxy, and superconductivity require release checks rather than assumed diagnostic thresholds. **Source:** B012.

### Experimental Task 3: Stage II — Premeasure constituents and laminate registered face-to-face stacks

#### Specific Content

##### C. Premeasured constituents → lamination → transport
1. Complete the individual angular transport measurements described below. Save untouched raw curves and mark the gauge region physically.
2. Qualify cleaning, flux removal, wetting and pressure on witnesses. Place superconducting faces inward with solder between qualified conductive surfaces; substrates face outward. Align coupons while maintaining registered gauge regions.
3. Laminate using the table. At 5 cm/min, 30 s dwell requires a 2.5 cm effective hot zone; measure actual material temperature and hot-zone length. Pressure is 0.5 MPa, not 0.5 kgf/cm², which equals approximately 0.049 MPa.
4. Cool under supported alignment to below 50 °C before winding or handling. Remove flux using its validated cleaning procedure. Do not add the draft's unqualified postbond anneal.
5. Measure bond thickness at ≥10 witness positions by calibrated cross-sectional microscopy. Inspect continuity, voids, edge cracking and delamination. Map electrical transfer along the witness. Hand off assembled IDs, constituent linkage, process traces, bond maps and disposition.

#### Related Knowledge

- Individual angular measurements must precede assembly, with untouched raw curves and physically marked gauge regions. The transport procedure supplies the common measurement basis for constituents and stacks. **Source:** B013, B018.
- Face-to-face assembly places solder between qualified conductive surfaces while substrates remain outward. Witness qualification of cleaning, wetting, pressure, thermal exposure, and alignment precedes processing core samples. **Source:** B004, B008, B013.
- Actual material temperature and hot-zone geometry must support the proposed dwell. Bond microscopy and electrical-transfer mapping provide the assembly hand-off evidence; an unqualified postbond anneal is excluded. **Source:** B013.

### Experimental Task 4: Stage II — Characterize texture and embedded BHO, and apply structural gates

#### Specific Content

##### 4. Structural characterization and gates
Perform θ–2θ scans, pole figures and the task's LMO 200 φ-scan. Verify indexing, scattering angle and specimen tilt using the actual epitaxial orientation relationship. Symmetric 002 reflections cannot substitute for an azimuthally sensitive in-plane scan. Use appropriate off-axis REBCO reflections to verify epitaxy; report film texture separately from LMO texture.

On E witnesses, acquire cross-sectional and plan-view TEM from ≥3 separated regions and quantify ≥100 rods where available: diameter, length, orientation to local c-axis, areal density and clustering. Identify BHO with diffraction plus Hf-sensitive chemistry; contrast or SEM-EDS alone cannot resolve/identify every nanoscale rod. Use low-energy final FIB polishing and check beam-damage artifacts. Report distributions, sampling limitations and calibration uncertainty, not assumed dimensions.

For measured plan-view density n, matching field is Bφ = nΦ₀, Φ₀ = 2.068×10⁻¹⁵ Wb. Thus 500–1000 rods/µm² corresponds to 1.03–2.07 T; do not use the draft's inconsistent range or infer Bφ from angular peak width alone.

Hold samples lacking identifiable embedded BHO rods or showing major texture/continuity loss. Record all failures and retain witnesses; corrective growth requires a new traceable lot.

#### Related Knowledge

- LMO in-plane texture and REBCO epitaxy require separately appropriate diffraction measurements. Symmetric reflections do not replace an azimuthally sensitive in-plane scan. **Source:** B014.
- Witness TEM sampling, diffraction, and Hf-sensitive chemistry support identification and quantification of embedded BHO rods. Report distributions, sampling limitations, and uncertainty instead of assuming dimensions from contrast or target morphology. **Source:** B011, B015.
- Matching field is calculated from measured plan-view density, not angular peak width alone. Missing identifiable embedded rods or major texture/continuity loss requires a hold, retained failure records, and a new traceable lot for corrective growth. **Source:** B016, B017.

### Experimental Task 5: Stage III — Perform qualified four-probe angular transport and current normalization

#### Specific Content

##### 5. Four-probe angular transport
1. Use robust current terminals, not fine voltage wires for current delivery. Connect both FFDS tapes in parallel at both ends. Place voltage taps 5 mm apart inside the nominal 20 mm current-terminal span; add tape-specific witness taps for transfer diagnostics. Verify that transfer regions do not invade the gauge length; lengthen specimens if necessary.
2. Mount without bending strain. Rotate about the tape-length axis so B remains perpendicular to current; θ = 0° is B//c, θ = 90° is B//ab. Calibrate zero, rotation sign and field uniformity over the gauge region.
3. Cool with monitored thermal gradients; stabilize at 65 K and 1.5 T. At zero current, rotate to each angle, settle until table tolerances are met, then acquire an I–V sweep. Use coarse steps followed by approximately 0.1 A steps near onset if resolution permits. Calibrate averaging time, ramp rate or pulse width to eliminate heating and inductive artifacts.
4. Subtract measured offsets using current reversal where safe. The 5 mm gauge gives Vcriterion = 0.5 µV. Verify effective noise well below this threshold. Set interlocked voltage, temperature and current abort limits from witness trials; stop after sufficient onset data, not into sustained quench.
5. Repeat the angular sweep in reverse order; repeat selected extrema. Apply identical conditions and extraction to Y, E and F. Never report source-limited curves as measured Ic.

**Capacity check:** 4 mm × 1 µm gives superconducting area 4×10⁻⁵ cm² per tape. The draft's hypothetical 3–4 MA/cm² would require 120–160 A per tape and 240–320 A for two tapes; these are sizing illustrations, not predictions at 65 K, 1.5 T. A 100 A source and 50 A sweep cannot be presumed adequate. Qualify a supply, leads and contacts that reach actual stack onset with safe headroom. Calculate single-film Jc = Ic/(wt); distinguish combined superconducting-area Jc from engineering current density using the full stack cross-section.

#### Related Knowledge

- Parallel end connections, robust current terminals, calibrated angular conventions, and gauge placement outside transfer regions establish the specified FFDS transport geometry. **Source:** B018.
- Noise verification, offset handling, witness-derived abort limits, reverse sweeps, and repeated extrema support consistent onset extraction without sustained quench. Source-limited curves cannot be reported as measured critical currents. **Source:** B018.
- Supply and contact capacity must reach actual stack onset with qualified headroom. Current-density reporting must use measured superconducting dimensions and distinguish combined superconducting area from full-stack engineering area. **Source:** B019, B008.

### Experimental Task 6: Stage III — Analyze interface transfer and angular comparisons, and archive dispositions

#### Specific Content

##### 6. Interface model, analysis and controlled feedback
Fit a documented two-branch equivalent circuit with percolation-transition Rsc,j(Ij,θ,T) and constant Rc. First constrain individual branch transitions with measured single-tape curves. Freeze circuit topology, transition equation, terminal geometry and fit range before comparing stacks. Report resistances in Ω and interface specific resistance ρc = RcAeff in Ω cm² only where effective transfer area is established; otherwise use a distributed transfer model.

The approximately 2 nΩ cm² interface value is a target. For a hypothetical uniform 4 mm × 30 mm area, Rc ≈1.67 nΩ; at 1 mA the signal is only 1.67 pV. Reject the draft's 1 mA verification method. Qualify transfer-sensitive contacts, reversible higher current and thermal controls. If stack-only I–V cannot identify Rc independently, obtain witness transfer data or report bounds rather than a unique fit.

Calculate Ic,F/(Ic,Y+Ic,E), minimum Ic and population σ(Ic)/mean(Ic). Use uniform 0°–175° points for the primary proposed statistic; retain the full sweep to assess symmetry/hysteresis. The supplied statistic's original angular weighting is unknown. Report propagated uncertainty and all pair results without smoothing away extrema. A >10% summation mismatch or >5% heat-witness Ic loss is a proposed investigation trigger, not automatic proof of interface failure.

Diagnose texture loss through template maps, weak c-axis response through rod/oxygenation data, and poor summation through transfer resistance, terminals, gauge placement, voids and heating. Change one qualified process factor at a time on witnesses; repeat all three core arms for a changed lot. Do not discard unexplained outliers.

##### 7. Safety, records and optional work
Use laser interlocks, oxygen-compatible furnace plumbing, solvent-rated milling controls, lead/flux extraction and hazardous-waste collection. Guard hot rollers and moving reels. Enforce magnet exclusion zones, cryogen ventilation/oxygen monitoring, pressure relief and automatic high-current shutdown.

Archive raw I–V, microscopy and diffraction files; calibration records; dimensions/tap coordinates; timestamps; process histories; field/temperature traces; fit code, residuals and uncertainty; failures and release signatures. Maintain backed-up read-only raw data.

Optional same-template undoped EuBCO controls can isolate composition effects; optional thermal cycling/peel tests use witnesses. Neither replaces the Y, E and FFDS comparison or establishes coil/high-field performance beyond the tested conditions.

#### Related Knowledge

- Measured constituent curves constrain the two-branch interface model. Specific interface resistance requires an established effective transfer area; insufficient identifiability requires witness data or bounds rather than a unique fitted value. **Source:** B020, B021.
- Current-summation ratios, minimum critical current, and population angular variation provide the comparison outputs. Uniform primary angular weighting, uncertainty propagation, and retained extrema prevent target values or investigation triggers from becoming asserted results. **Source:** B022, B023.
- Safety prerequisites and read-only raw-data preservation apply throughout execution. Optional controls and witness durability tests do not replace the core comparison or establish performance outside the tested conditions. **Source:** B024, B025, B026.
