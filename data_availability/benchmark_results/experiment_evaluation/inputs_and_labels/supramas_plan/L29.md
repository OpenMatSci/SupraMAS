# Experimental Plan: YBCO temperature-transfer and field-prediction protocol

## Experimental Draft

### Stage I — Qualify the comparison and fabricate films
Reconstruct the temperature-transfer study for undoped, c-axis epitaxial YBCO on CeO₂-terminated IBAD-MgO metallic templates and single-crystal SrTiO₃. Compare four PLD systems, documenting static or reel-to-reel configurations. The proposed core comprises three temperature levels and three independent depositions per chamber/substrate cell, totaling 72 films where mounting compatibility permits. Proposed 860/880/900 °C levels require thermal calibration, heater capability and stack-stability approval. Keep non-temperature recipes fixed within each chamber, randomize feasible run blocks, retain baseline repeats and lock prediction splits before fitting.

Use nominal 1:2:3 ceramic targets, qualified substrates, oxygen-compatible PLD and annealing equipment, and calibrated temperature, pressure and deposition-dose measurements. Resolve the 246 versus 248 nm laser discrepancy before release. Witness qualification precedes recipe freezing; proposed settings are not established operating conditions. Fabricate 200–250 nm films, preserve commercial buffers, record plume images qualitatively, and apply the common 400 °C flowing-oxygen anneal with qualified dwell, flow and cooling.

### Stage II — Qualify films and prepare transport bridges
Combine thickness mapping, optical/SEM inspection, AFM and XRD, including off-axis texture measurements. Release intact films with dominant c-axis epitaxy; retain nonconforming records without excluding low-Jc specimens. Prepare dimensioned bridges with isolated contacts and independently measured voltage-tap separation.

### Stage III — Measure transport and develop predictions
Qualify current capability, nanovoltage sensitivity, thermal anchoring and field alignment before measuring Tc and transport at 65 and 77 K over 0–9 T. Normalize Ic using measured cross-section and the fixed electric-field criterion. Compare GPR with proposed temperature-coordinate transfer learning, then qualify the proposed 1/Jc³ field model before withheld predictions. Outputs include traceable measurements, uncertainties, failures and prediction intervals. Jc > 3 MA cm⁻² and held-out R² ≥ 0.81 remain reference targets, not measured outcomes; natural-defect interpretations remain noncausal unless otherwise supported.

## Detailed Experimental Tasks

### Experimental Task 1: Stage I — Define the comparison and qualify PLD resources and operating conditions

#### Specific Content

##### 1. Scope and experimental matrix
Propose undoped, c-axis epitaxial YBCO films on CeO₂-terminated IBAD-MgO metallic templates and single-crystal SrTiO₃ (STO). Optimize substrate temperature across four PLD systems using Gaussian-process regression (GPR), transfer learning (TL), and physics-aided modeling (PAM) of 1/Jc³. Natural defects are explanatory variables, not deliberately introduced treatments. Targets—not measured outcomes—are Jc > 3 MA cm⁻² at 77 K self-field and held-out R² ≥ 0.81 for Jc predictions at 65 K through 3 T.

Assign chambers C1–C4 and record their actual static/reel-to-reel configuration. IDs: `C#-I/S-T###-R#`, with I = IBAD/CeO₂, S = STO, T = temperature setpoint, R = independent deposition. Append `-W`, `-B1`, or `-TEM` for witness, transport bridge, or destructive specimen. Link tape coupons to web position and exposure history.

| Comparison | Proposed allocation | Control rule |
|---|---|---|
| Core | 4 chambers × 2 substrates × 3 temperatures × 3 depositions = 72 films | Independently deposited replicates, not multiple bridges from one film |
| Initial temperatures | 860, 880, 900 °C | Proposed physical-temperature levels, subject to thermal calibration and stack stability |
| Baseline | Middle-temperature sample in each chamber/substrate cell | Repeat across run blocks to detect drift |
| Prediction test | One replicate per cell held out; one chamber treated as destination in each transfer fold | Lock split before fitting |

Randomize temperature order within feasible run blocks; balance substrate lots and run days. Verify both substrates can be mounted in every chamber. If crossing is impossible, document missing cells and restrict inference to connected comparisons; do not substitute a different substrate. Do not change oxygen pressure, composition, texture, or thickness during the core temperature sweep.

##### 2. Materials, equipment and release prerequisites
Use undoped nominal Y:Ba:Cu = 1:2:3 ceramic targets, preferably one certified lot; qualified CeO₂-terminated IBAD-MgO metal templates with Δϕ ≤ 2°; atomically flat STO with documented orientation/miscut; high-purity oxygen; compatible solvents; lithography materials and isolated metal contact pads. Do not add excess CuO, BZO, BHO, LAO, or intentional defect-forming treatments.

Required: four PLD systems with temperature and oxygen-pressure monitoring; optical plume camera; oxygen-compatible annealing furnace; XRD with off-axis capability; thickness/width metrology; optical microscope, SEM and preferably AFM; lithography/contact deposition equipment; PPMS for Tc; four-terminal transport system capable of 65–77 K, 9 T and adequate current, voltage sensitivity and thermal anchoring. TEM/FIB is optional.

Before release, resolve the task's 246 nm versus draft's 248 nm KrF specification against equipment documentation. Verify heaters can sustain the approved ≥860 °C range: a heater limited to 800 °C cannot execute it. Calibrate surface temperature against heater readout, including emissivity, mounting and moving-web effects. Retain both raw and corrected temperatures; TL must not conceal calibration errors.

##### 3. Consolidated operating conditions
All entries marked **P** are proposed choices requiring witness calibration and approval, not task-supplied settings. Freeze the approved recipe before core fabrication.

| Parameter | Core setting or release requirement |
|---|---|
| YBCO thickness | 200–250 nm; nominal 225 nm |
| Surface temperature | P: 860/880/900 °C; stability ±5 °C; uncertainty and uniformity measured |
| Growth oxygen pressure | P: 300 ±15 mTorr = 40.0 ±2.0 Pa, measured with calibrated pressure gauge |
| Fluence at target | P: 2.5 ±0.25 J cm⁻²; measure illuminated spot and pulse energy |
| Pulse rate | P: 8 Hz; chamber-specific exposure geometry documented |
| Static target distance | P: 50 ±1 mm where supported; otherwise freeze verified chamber geometry |
| Conditioning | P: base pressure ≤5×10⁻⁶ Torr; shuttered pre-ablation, 500 pulses |
| Deposition dose | Calibrate nm/pulse or web exposure using witnesses; do not assume equal dose between chambers |
| Oxygen anneal | 400 °C; P: ±5 °C, 30 ±2 min, 200 ±10 sccm at ambient pressure in an approved furnace |
| Cooling | P: 5 ±1 °C min⁻¹ under oxygen; qualify stack integrity |
| Transport | 65 and 77 K; P: stability ±0.1 K; B∥c within ±0.5° |
| Current criterion | 1 µV cm⁻¹, fixed for all comparisons |

If nominal fluence or geometry cannot be shared, retain stable chamber-specific settings and include them in metadata. Commissioning may adjust these settings using separate witnesses, but changes start a new controlled cohort.

#### Related Knowledge

- Independent depositions, repeated middle-temperature controls and locked holdouts define the comparison; missing substrate/chamber crossings restrict inference to connected comparisons rather than justify substrate substitution. **Source:** B001, B002, B003.
- Surface-temperature calibration, heater capability and resolution of the laser-wavelength discrepancy are release prerequisites. Proposed operating settings require witness approval, and chamber-specific dose or geometry cannot be assumed equivalent. **Source:** B006, B007, B008, B009.
- The core material is undoped YBCO on the two specified substrate architectures. Natural defects are explanatory variables, and the supplied performance values are targets rather than observations. **Source:** B000, B004.

### Experimental Task 2: Stage I — Qualify substrates, deposit YBCO and complete oxygenation hand-off

#### Specific Content

##### 4. Sequential fabrication and hand-offs
1. **Receive and qualify substrates.** Log supplier, metal/buffer stack, lot, dimensions, surface orientation and existing texture data. Measure IBAD template off-axis φ width and inspect both substrates for scratches, contamination and damage. Use supplier-qualified cleaning; proposed starting treatment is acetone then isopropanol, 10 min each, nitrogen drying. Validate ultrasonication on sacrificial templates first. Do not routinely apply plasma, high-temperature annealing or acid polishing to completed buffers. STO surface preparation requires an approved recipe and AFM confirmation before use.
2. **Release to PLD.** Characterization operator signs substrate acceptance. Preserve commercial buffer architecture; do not redeposit CeO₂ merely to match an unsupported thickness. Load with clean fixtures; record substrate contact, heater position and tape tension where applicable.
3. **Condition chamber and target.** Check vacuum, oxygen delivery, interlocks, target condition and laser fluence. Rotate/raster target using a fixed qualified pattern. Pre-ablate behind a closed shutter. Establish oxygen pressure and temperature; wait until the approved stability requirement is met.
4. **Deposit.** Open shutter or begin qualified web exposure. Record pulses, exposure time, web speed/passes, temperature and pressure traces. Acquire plume images with documented camera geometry, gain and exposure. Use imaging qualitatively; camera brightness alone is not deposition flux or evidence of uniformity.
5. **Oxygenate and cool.** Stop ablation, cool under the approved oxygen schedule, and perform the common 400 °C flowing-oxygen dwell in an approved furnace. If transfer is necessary, first cool safely, identify the carrier and record air exposure and transfer duration. Do not pressurize a PLD chamber to atmospheric oxygen unless explicitly rated for that operation.
6. **Hand off to characterization.** Photograph each film, label storage carrier, and provide the complete deposition/anneal record. Keep films clean and dry; do not destructively etch transport specimens for defect counting.

#### Related Knowledge

- Substrate acceptance precedes loading; supplier-qualified cleaning and approved STO preparation preserve the incoming architecture without unsupported buffer redeposition or routine aggressive treatments. **Source:** B010.
- Deposition requires stable approved conditions and recorded exposure histories. Plume images support qualitative comparison only, while oxygenation transfers require safe cooling and documented air exposure. **Source:** B010.

### Experimental Task 3: Stage II — Measure film structure and apply the specimen quality gate

#### Specific Content

##### 5. Structural qualification and bridge preparation
Measure thickness at mapped positions by calibrated profilometry, reflectometry or a validated witness method. Inspect optical/SEM morphology for cracks, delamination and droplets; obtain AFM roughness with scan area recorded.

Collect ω–2θ scans to identify YBCO (00l), secondary phases and unwanted orientations. Measure a separate rocking curve, for example at (005). For in-plane texture use an accessible **off-axis** reflection, such as YBCO (103) or MgO (111), with verified geometry and overlap checks. A symmetric MgO (002) φ-scan does not determine in-plane mosaic spread. Report reflection, radiation wavelength, instrument broadening, scan geometry, all peak widths and uncertainty.

For (005), d005 = c/5 and c = 5λ/(2 sin θ); θ is half the measured 2θ. Report c in Å. Do not infer quantitative dislocation density from XRD width alone or interpret c shifts solely as strain: oxygen content also affects lattice parameters.

**Quality gate:** release intact, thickness-qualified films with confirmed dominant c-axis epitaxy. Flag nonconforming specimens, retain their records, and quarantine unsafe/delaminated films. Low Jc is not an exclusion criterion. Do not impose unsupported defect-density or lattice-parameter targets.

#### Related Knowledge

- Mapped thickness and surface inspection establish specimen dimensions and integrity; dominant c-axis epitaxy is checked by diffraction, with off-axis geometry required for in-plane texture. **Source:** B011, B012, B014.
- The (005) spacing supports calculation of the c-axis parameter, but lattice shifts cannot be attributed solely to strain and XRD widths do not independently quantify dislocation density. **Source:** B013.
- The release gate concerns integrity, thickness and epitaxy. Low Jc is not an exclusion criterion, and nonconforming or unsafe specimens retain documented dispositions. **Source:** B014.

### Experimental Task 4: Stage II — Pattern bridges and verify contact and measurement geometry

#### Specific Content

Pattern nominal 50 µm × 3 mm bridges using a YBCO-compatible lithography/etch process qualified on witnesses. Verify actual width, edge damage, film thickness and voltage-tap separation L. Add proposed isolated 100 nm Au pads without a continuous shunting layer between taps. Current contacts must lie outside voltage taps. Inspect continuity and room-temperature contact behavior. Hand off the mounted, dimensioned bridge and geometry image to transport staff.

#### Related Knowledge

- Witness-qualified lithography and verification of actual width, thickness, edge condition and voltage-tap separation provide the geometry needed for transport normalization. **Source:** B015.
- Isolated contact pads avoid a continuous shunting layer between voltage taps; current contacts remain outside the taps, with continuity and room-temperature contact behavior checked before hand-off. **Source:** B015.

### Experimental Task 5: Stage III — Qualify transport capability and measure Tc and field-dependent Jc

#### Specific Content

##### 6. Transport operations and consistency checks
At w = 50 µm = 0.005 cm and t = 200–250 nm = (2.0–2.5)×10⁻⁵ cm, superconducting cross-section is (1.0–1.25)×10⁻⁷ cm². The 3 MA cm⁻² target therefore requires Ic = 0.300–0.375 A. The draft's 100 mA source cannot reach it. Proposed procurement/qualification is ≥1 A capability, with verified contacts, leads, thermal limits and compliance; higher Jc or lower temperature may require more. Record current-limited curves as censored, never as measured Ic.

Vc = EcL. If L = 3 mm = 0.3 cm, Vc = **0.3 µV**, not 3 µV. Bridge length is not automatically tap separation. Require demonstrated voltage noise/offset substantially below Vc; proposed acceptance is ≤0.03 µV after averaging for this geometry. A 1 µV-resolution meter is inadequate without verified finer effective sensitivity.

1. Mount with compatible thermal anchoring, twisted voltage pairs and documented B∥c alignment. Verify magnet range, calibrated field, current capability and nanovoltage performance; PPMS naming alone does not establish these capabilities.
2. Measure zero-field R(T), proposed 100→60 K at ≤1 K min⁻¹ with sufficiently low sensing current to avoid heating. Record onset, midpoint, zero-resistance definition, transition width and current. Confirm Tc exceeds the transport temperature.
3. Zero-field cool to each target temperature; document magnet remanence. Proposed field grid: 0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9 T. Stabilize temperature and field before each sweep. Use the same ascending-field history; repeat selected descending points to check hysteresis.
4. Acquire three repeat I–V sweeps per point, with current reversal/offset checks and dwell/current steps calibrated below threshold. Stop on heating, excessive voltage or compliance. Determine Ic by interpolation at Vc; calculate Jc = Ic/(wt). Report uncertainty from current, dimensions and criterion extraction. Applied 0 T denotes self-field conditions, not absence of the sample's magnetic field.

#### Related Knowledge

- The specified film cross-section makes the reference Jc target require more current than a 100 mA source supplies. Current capability and thermal limits must be qualified, and compliance-limited curves remain censored. **Source:** B016.
- The voltage threshold follows Vc = EcL using measured tap separation. For 3 mm separation it is 0.3 µV, requiring demonstrated noise and offset substantially below that threshold. **Source:** B017.
- Consistent field history, verified alignment, stabilized temperature and repeated I–V sweeps support comparable Ic extraction. Jc uses measured width and thickness, with dimensional and extraction uncertainties retained. **Source:** B018.

### Experimental Task 6: Stage III — Consolidate provenance, qualify predictive models and disposition follow-up

#### Specific Content

##### 7. Modeling, feedback and dispositions
Store raw I–V and R(T), unsuccessful runs, dimensions, uncertainty, XRD files, images, calibration IDs, substrate lot, chamber geometry, plume settings and complete time traces. Keep specimen-level provenance and exclusion reasons.

Fit reference-field Jc versus temperature with GPR, representing substrate and chamber as categorical/domain information. Proposed TL uses a regularized affine temperature-coordinate map per chamber, learned from overlapping training data; fix the reference chamber map to identity. Compare against a no-transfer GPR using identical splits. Report mapping uncertainty and confounding, not just R².

For PAM, transform positive Jc to y = 1/Jc³ with fixed units and propagated uncertainty. The task does not supply the field equation. A proposed starting model is y(B) = a + bB, constrained positive, calibrated on designated training field curves. Test residuals and low-field crossover separately; do not assume validity near self-field. One reference-field value cannot identify both coefficients without a separately established relation. Freeze the learned field relation before predicting withheld 65 K points through 3 T; report R², errors and prediction intervals in Jc space. Fields above 3 T test extrapolation and remain separate from the stated prediction objective.

For low Tc, verify thermometer and oxygenation; qualify longer dwell on separate witnesses. For cracks, check cooling, mounting and temperature uniformity. For poor texture, inspect incoming templates and epitaxy. For low Jc, first check geometry, contacts, heating and criterion; never lower the criterion to improve the comparison. For noisy voltages, improve shielding/averaging and repeat qualification. For poor model transfer, inspect domain coverage and calibration before adding complexity.

Select new temperatures by uncertainty-aware acquisition within the qualified range; retain baseline controls and test new independent films. Any other recipe change requires a separately labeled cohort. Optional TEM on matched sacrificial specimens may test dislocation/boundary interpretations, but correlations alone do not establish pinning causality. No doping or unrelated mechanical-testing module belongs to the core experiment.

##### 8. Risk controls
Follow interlocked UV-laser, hot-surface, vacuum and oxygen-service procedures. Keep solvents away from oxygen/hot equipment; use ventilation and appropriate waste streams for lithography. Control cryogen/asphyxiation and high-field projectile hazards. Verify electrical compliance and quench/heating aborts before high-current testing. Handle FIB/TEM specimens destructively only after transport or from linked witnesses.

#### Related Knowledge

- Specimen-level raw data and calibration provenance support comparison of no-transfer GPR with the proposed regularized chamber temperature map using identical locked splits; transfer must not conceal calibration errors. **Source:** B019, B020, B006.
- The proposed positive linear field relation for 1/Jc³ requires training-curve calibration and residual checks. A single reference-field value cannot identify both coefficients without an independently established relation, and self-field validity is unresolved. **Source:** B021.
- Failure checks precede recipe changes, which require separate witnesses or cohorts. Optional destructive microscopy does not establish pinning causality, and laser, oxygen, cryogenic, magnetic and electrical prerequisites remain mandatory. **Source:** B022, B023, B024.
