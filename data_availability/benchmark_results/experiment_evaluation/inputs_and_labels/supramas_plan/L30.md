# Experimental Plan: EuBCO–BHO coated-conductor protocol

## Experimental Draft

### Reconstructed experimental overview

Test the trade-off between in-field critical current density and magnetic vortex creep in PLD EuBCO containing in-situ BHO inclusions on qualified IBAD-MgO buffered Hastelloy. Preserve four nominal BHO arms: 0, 1.9, 2.8 and 4.8 vol%, with three independent growths per arm proposed. Undoped films require an actual substrate temperature of 1150 °C; BHO-containing films require 1055 °C. This temperature difference prevents a composition-only causal interpretation.

Use Eu2O3, BaCO3, CuO and BHO to prepare composite targets, not sequential EuBCO/BHO layers. Required resources include qualified prebuffered tape, powder-processing and oxygen-furnace equipment, calibrated KrF PLD, XRD, SEM/EDS, surface metrology, FIB, TEM/STEM/EDS and MPMS3. Before processing, confirm the supplier’s stack order and thermal qualification, hardware compatibility, actual substrate thermometry and installed magnetometer capabilities.

The workflow comprises qualification and comparison setup, target-to-film fabrication and coupon release, structural characterization, magnetic hysteresis and relaxation analysis, and optional transport with final disposition. Preserve sample lineage, randomize growth and measurement order within replicate blocks, and process matching blank substrates for magnetic backgrounds. Proposed process settings, including 300 nm thickness and 300 °C/2 h oxygenation, remain calibration starting points until pilot qualification.

Measure inclusion morphology and actual film BHO fraction rather than assuming target loading transfers directly or imposing rods on every arm. Normalize magnetic moments to superconducting film volume, collect common-temperature hysteresis loops, and perform the core one-hour relaxation measurement at 30 K, 1 T. Optional transport uses sibling coupons and does not replace magnetic creep measurements.

Anticipated outputs are traceable morphology, magnetic Jc and creep comparisons with uncertainties, qualification records and documented failures. Supplied performance values remain reference targets, not measured outcomes or release criteria.

## Detailed Experimental Tasks

### Experimental Task 1: Establish comparison arms and qualify materials, equipment and process settings

#### Specific Content

##### 1. Objective and comparison design
Test the trade-off between in-field critical current density and vortex creep in PLD EuBCO containing coherent BHO inclusions. Preserve the undoped correlated-disorder control and the 1.9, 2.8 and 4.8 vol% arms. Supplied reference features are approximately 4.5 nm particles at 1.9 vol%, splayed rods at 4.8 vol%, and approximately 20 nm Eu2O3 precipitates, grain-boundary c-axis dislocations and twins in undoped EuBCO. Measure these features; do not impose identical rod morphology on every arm.

| Arm | Nominal BHO, vol% | Task growth temperature | Independent growths, proposed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| B00 | 0 | 1150 °C | 3 |
| B19 | 1.9 | 1055 °C | 3 |
| B28 | 2.8 | 1055 °C | 3 |

Use identifiers `E-Bxx-Rnn`, then suffixes `-M` magnetic, `-X` microscopy and `-T` optional transport. Retain parent-to-coupon coordinates. Randomize growth and measurement order within replicate blocks. Include a matching blank buffered substrate, `BL-Rnn`, through the thermal/O2 treatment for magnetic-background checks. Record actual film BHO fraction separately from nominal target loading.

The temperature difference between undoped and doped growths is part of the required comparison and confounds a composition-only causal interpretation.

##### 2. Materials, equipment and release prerequisites
Use Eu2O3, BaCO3, CuO, BHO powder, ethanol, oxygen and qualified IBAD-MgO buffered Hastelloy containing Gd2Zr2O7, Y2O3, LaMnO3 and CeO2. Use composite EuBCO–BHO PLD targets for simultaneous in-situ incorporation, not sequential BHO layers.

Equipment: powder handling enclosure, balance, mill, press, controlled-O2 furnace, KrF PLD with calibrated substrate thermometry and pressure gauges, XRD, SEM/EDS, AFM or profilometry, FIB, TEM/HAADF-STEM/EDS, and MPMS3. Optional transport requires lithography, isolated contacts, nanovoltmeter, suitably rated current source and field-capable cryostat.

Before processing, obtain the substrate supplier's substrate-to-film stack order, thicknesses and thermal qualification. The supplied slash-separated buffer list is not an executable deposition sequence. Use qualified prebuffered tape; do not polish or ion-clean its finished cap without validation. In-house buffer production requires a separately approved stack-specific recipe.

Verify PLD hardware and substrate survival at the stated **actual substrate** temperatures. Do not substitute a heater setting or reinterpret °C as another unit. Verify the installed MPMS3 reaches 1.8 K and at least ±5 T; the draft's ±7 T capability must be checked on the actual instrument. Verify sample fit, moment range, field history control and temperature stability.

### Experimental Task 2: Prepare and release targets, grow and oxygenate films, and divide indexed coupons

#### Specific Content

##### 4. Sequential fabrication and hand-offs
1. **Prepare matrix powder.** Weigh Eu2O3:BaCO3:CuO in the corrected molar ratio **0.5:2:3**, giving Eu:Ba:Cu = 1:2:3. Select batch mass from the die requirement, not the draft's arbitrary gram quantities. Mix, dry, calcine and regrind using the starting recipe. Screen by XRD for matrix formation and residual carbonate/reaction products.
2. **Add BHO once.** Divide qualified matrix powder among arms and blend BHO only at this stage. For target fraction f, use `mBHO/mEuBCO = [f/(1−f)](rhoBHO/rhoEuBCO)` with documented phase densities. Target loading is not assumed equal to deposited film volume fraction. Press and sinter targets; log density, cracks, composition and batch lineage.
3. **Release targets to PLD.** Use XRD and polished-section SEM/EDS to establish expected EuBCO/BHO phases and Hf homogeneity. Quarantine cracked targets or unexplained reaction products; refine processing before growth. Do not apply a blanket secondary-phase exclusion that would conceal the task's intrinsic Eu2O3 population.
4. **Release templates.** Map cap morphology and texture. Validate solvent cleaning on sacrificial buffered tape before using it; dry with nitrogen and handle only edges. Reject delamination or contamination. Transfer template certificate and cleaning log with each sample.
5. **Grow nanocomposites.** Mount matched templates consistently. Stabilize calibrated temperature and O2 pressure; pre-ablate with shutter closed using the qualified procedure. Deposit rotating/rastered composite targets under frozen settings. Use calibrated pulse counts for equal thickness. Record plume changes, excursions and target erosion. RHEED, if available, monitors growth; pressure is measured by a gauge.
6. **Oxygenate.** Cool under the qualified oxygen-preserving schedule, then transfer to the O2 furnace for the proposed 300 °C/2 h treatment. Use an approved pressure transition and gas flow. Omit the contradictory additional 500 °C hold unless separately qualified. Log complete temperature/pressure histories.
7. **Release and divide films.** Inspect SEM/XRD and measure thickness at several positions. Quarantine cracks, delamination, severe nonuniformity or unexpected phases. Retain anomalous samples and reasons; do not discard solely for low Jc. Cut indexed sibling coupons, inspect edge damage and hand magnetic coupons to magnetometry before any contact processing. Send sacrificial coupons to microscopy.

#### Related Knowledge

- The corrected precursor ratio produces the specified matrix stoichiometry. BHO is added once to qualified matrix powder using documented phase densities; nominal target loading does not establish deposited film volume fraction. **Source:** B011.
- Target phase and homogeneity checks, template qualification, calibrated pulse counts and complete thermal/pressure histories provide the sequential release gates for fabrication. **Source:** B011.
- Film release retains anomalies and failure reasons rather than excluding samples solely for low Jc. Indexed sibling coupons preserve parent coordinates, with magnetic coupons handed off before contact processing. **Source:** B002, B011.

### Experimental Task 3: Characterize film texture, inclusion morphology and intrinsic defects

#### Specific Content

##### 5. Structural and compositional characterization
Use XRD θ–2θ scans, EuBCO (00l) rocking curves and asymmetric reflections for in-plane texture. Symmetric cubic MgO/CeO2 (002) reflections cannot provide a conventional fourfold φ-scan in a (001)-textured specimen; use accessible {111} or other verified asymmetric reflections. Use an accessible EuBCO asymmetric reflection such as (103), verifying peak overlap, indices and angles with calibrated Cu-Kα wavelength and reference structures. Record angles in degrees and spacings in nm or Å consistently.

SEM: proposed 10 kV imaging at five distributed locations; EDS screens larger surface particles but cannot resolve 4.5 nm inclusions reliably. AFM: proposed 5 × 5 µm maps.

Prepare cross-sectional and plan-view FIB specimens from indexed locations. Apply a protective cap, rough-mill at 30 kV, progressively reduce current, and finish at ≤2 kV. Calibrate specimen thickness and damage; retain undamaged comparison regions. Transfer lamella IDs and coordinates to TEM.

At proposed 200 kV, acquire HAADF-STEM, diffraction/high-resolution interface images and EDS maps including Eu, Ba, Hf, Cu and O. Establish coherence/orientation from lattice imaging/diffraction, not EDS alone. Quantify diameter, length, aspect ratio, splay relative to c, spacing, inclusion fraction, grain size, Eu2O3, twins and grain-boundary dislocations. Proposed sampling: ≥50 inclusions when available and ≥10 distributed defect fields per growth. Use multiple diffraction vectors to avoid dislocation invisibility bias. State stereological assumptions and uncertainty; do not substitute rod areal density for volume fraction. Optimize dose against beam damage rather than assuming an unvalidated universal dose limit.

#### Related Knowledge

- Texture analysis requires verified asymmetric reflections; symmetric cubic MgO/CeO2 (002) reflections do not supply the specified conventional fourfold phi-scan geometry. **Source:** B012.
- SEM/EDS screens surface features but cannot reliably resolve the supplied approximately 4.5 nm inclusions. Indexed cross-sectional and plan-view FIB specimens support the complementary TEM examination. **Source:** B013, B014.
- Lattice imaging and diffraction establish coherence and orientation, whereas elemental maps alone do not. Morphology and defect quantification retain sampling, stereological and damage uncertainties without assuming universal rod morphology. **Source:** B000, B015.

### Experimental Task 4: Measure magnetic hysteresis and relaxation and calculate Jc and creep parameters

#### Specific Content

##### 6. Magnetic measurements and analysis
Measure coupon dimensions a ≤ b in cm and superconducting thickness d in cm; film volume is abd, excluding buffers and metal. Measure processed blanks using identical mounting, centering and field histories. Preserve raw moments and subtract justified substrate/holder backgrounds branch-by-branch; hysteretic substrate signals require particular scrutiny.

Screen superconducting transition with low-field M(T), proposed 10 Oe (approximately 1 mT), over a range spanning the transition. Record ZFC/FC histories separately. Do not infer p = 0.152 from oxygenation temperature alone; any doping proxy requires EuBCO-specific calibration.

For H parallel c, collect identical full loops at **1.8, 20, 30, 40, 60 and 77 K**, resolving self-field, 1 T and 5 T. Use verified excursion beyond 5 T if needed to obtain both developed branches at 5 T; otherwise label endpoint/critical-state limitations. Record stabilization, sweep direction, rate and remanent-field correction.

For a rectangular thin film in perpendicular field, calculate `Jc = 20 ΔM/[a(1−a/(3b))]` in A/cm², with ΔM the full loop width in emu/cm³ of superconducting film. Document critical-state validity, demagnetization, edge effects and detection limits. This magnetic estimate has **no electric-field voltage criterion**. Fit `Jc ∝ B^(−alpha)` only over a common, justified field interval excluding self-field and unresolved signals.

For relaxation, thermally reset above Tc, cool reproducibly, establish the same critical-state history and approach the final field from the same direction. Define time zero at final-field arrival; log settling dead time. At **30 K, 1 T**, record magnetic moment for 3600 s, with dense early sampling. Proposed additional common temperatures: 20, 40 and 60 K at 1 T. Repeat the preparation for every run; monitor field/temperature drift and blank relaxation.

Compute `S = −d ln|Mirr|/d ln t` after background correction, using identical justified time windows. Fit `J(t)=J0[1+(mu T/U0)ln(t/t0)]^(−1/mu)` with U0 expressed in kelvin; use kBT if U0 is in energy units. Report fit bounds, residuals, covariance and sensitivity to dead time/t0. One-hour data may not identify U0, t0 and mu independently. Do not declare a Bose glass from mu near 7/9 alone or infer double-kink motion solely from increasing S.

#### Related Knowledge

- Magnetic normalization uses measured superconducting film volume, excluding buffers and metal. Processed blanks and branch-specific background treatment are necessary for interpreting film hysteresis. **Source:** B016, B019.
- Common loop temperatures, field histories and justified field intervals support comparisons across arms. Magnetic Bean estimates have no electric-field voltage criterion, and endpoint limitations must remain explicit. **Source:** B018, B019.
- Reproducible critical-state preparation, final-field time zero and settling records support comparable one-hour relaxation analyses. Collective-creep fits retain parameter-identifiability limits and do not establish a mechanism from a single fitted exponent or trend. **Source:** B020, B021.

### Experimental Task 5: Qualify optional transport and extensions, resolve failures and archive comparisons

#### Specific Content

##### 7. Optional transport and controlled extensions
Use sibling bridges, proposed width 50 µm and thickness 300 nm, with measured **voltage-tap separation** 500 µm. Validate etching and non-shunting contacts on sacrificial coupons; avoid an unqualified N2 contact anneal that changes oxygenation.

The cross-section is 1.5 × 10⁻⁷ cm². Thus 31, 2 and 3 MA/cm² require **4.65, 0.30 and 0.45 A**, respectively; 100 mA reaches only **0.667 MA/cm²**. Verify source, leads, contacts and cooling accordingly, or redesign the bridge and document new dimensions. At 1 µV/cm, 500 µm tap separation gives **50 nV**. Verify noise and thermoelectric-offset rejection below this level. Use bidirectional current sweeps and heating checks; calibrated pulses may be needed. Measure 77 K self-field first; extend to task points only with verified field/temperature/current capability. Fixed-current voltage decay is not the specified magnetic-creep measurement.

Optional H parallel ab/angle scans require verified rotation geometry and appropriate analysis, not the perpendicular-field Bean formula. Optional temperature-matched growth controls may assess the composition/temperature confound but must not replace core arms.

##### 8. Disposition, safety and feedback
Contain powders and solvents; interlock the UV laser, vacuum/pressure equipment and hot surfaces. Apply oxygen-compatible gas handling, cryogen ventilation, magnet exclusion zones and approved FIB/lithography chemical procedures.

For phase contamination, inspect precursor reaction and target transfer; for poor texture, inspect template quality and temperature calibration; for wrong BHO fraction, recalibrate loading-to-film transfer; for anomalous creep, check background, centering, settling and drift before invoking mechanisms. For transport heating/noise, stop and qualify contacts/current delivery.

Archive raw files, calibration records, geometries, target recipes, processing histories, micrographs, segmentation rules, fits and exclusions under sample IDs. Compare replicate-level Jc and S with uncertainties and measured morphology. Treat supplied Jc/S values as reference targets, not measured outcomes. Freeze the core recipe after pilots; change one factor at a time in documented follow-up blocks and repeat all affected controls.
