# Experimental Plan: GdBCO–BHO coated-conductor protocol

## Experimental Draft

### Objective and scope
Reconstruct the proposed comparison of nanoparticle-dispersed GdBCO produced by reel-to-reel fluorine-free MOD on purchased CeO2-buffered IBAD tape. Examine composition-dependent pinning, thickness scaling, texture sharpening, interfaces and thermal coarsening without substituting nanorods or discrete BHO layers. All experiments remain proposed; supplied performance values are reference targets, not measured outcomes.

### Comparison design
The core matrix compares BHO loadings of 0, 1.7 **P**, 2.7 and 3.4 mol % across one, four and nine complete cycles. Separate comparisons vary added CeO2 thickness between 100, 300 and 700 nm and compare independently measured buffer-texture groups. Use three independently coated replicates per coupon arm **P**, randomized coating order and lot-blocked analysis. Texture-group availability and the loading denominator require resolution; unavailable groups cannot support claims of independence from buffer misalignment.

### Resources and workflow
Use qualified Gd/Ba/Cu precursor, colloidal BHO, compatible solvents and the approved tape stack. Required capabilities include RF sputtering, controlled reel-to-reel coating and thermal zones, atmosphere monitoring, particle characterization, XRD, SEM, TEM/STEM-EDX, thickness metrology, cryogenic DC transport and THEVA Tapestar mapping.

Approve the traveler and qualify unresolved conditions before production. Inspect and modify the buffer, release verified dispersions, qualify thermal conversion, then repeat complete coating–drying–pyrolysis–crystallization–cooling cycles. Oxygenate completed films, characterize structure and measure full-width transport before selecting a locked recipe for 200 m scale-up.

### Constraints and anticipated outputs
**P** settings require calibration; **U** conditions block affected operations. Measure actual thickness, geometry and oxygen partial pressure rather than inferring them from cycles or gas flow. Anticipated outputs are traceable structural, transport and longitudinal comparison datasets, including uncertainty, failed arms and qualification records. Maintain solvent, nanoparticle, furnace, cryogenic, magnet and high-current safety prerequisites throughout.

## Detailed Experimental Tasks

### Experimental Task 1: Approve the comparison matrix and qualify materials, equipment and starting settings

#### Specific Content

##### 1. Scope and release prerequisites
Produce nanoparticle-dispersed GdBCO on CeO2-buffered IBAD tape by reel-to-reel fluorine-free MOD. Test composition-dependent pinning, thickness scaling, self-epitaxial texture sharpening, interfaces, and thermal coarsening. All experiments below are proposed; supplied performance values are reference targets.

Before production, approve a traveler defining the complete purchased IBAD stack, BHO mol-% denominator, precursor concentrations/solvent chemistry, nanoparticle preparation route, furnace atmosphere, and validated dwell profiles. Do not substitute another substrate, nanorods, or discrete BHO layers. IBAD-MgO is acceptable only if confirmed as the supplied tape stack.

Parameters marked **P** are proposed starting settings or acceptance limits requiring calibration; **U** means unresolved and blocks the relevant operation. Calibration coupons are not counted as comparison specimens.

##### 2. Identifiers and comparison matrix
Use `Lxx-Cxx-Bxxx-Xxx-Nxx-Rxx-Pxxxx`: precursor/tape lot, BHO loading, added CeO2 thickness in nm, measured buffer-texture group, cycle count, replicate, and longitudinal position. Link each destructive specimen to its parent tape.

| Comparison | Conditions held fixed | Arms |
|---|---|---|
| Composition × thickness, core | Same IBAD lot; 300 nm added CeO2; qualified thermal recipe | BHO 0, 1.7 **P**, 2.7, 3.4 mol % × 1, 4, 9 complete cycles |
| Interface, core | Same buffer-texture group; four cycles; identical thermal history | Added CeO2 100, 300, 700 nm × BHO 0 and 2.7 mol % |
| Self-epitaxy, core | 300 nm added CeO2; one cycle; identical chemistry/history | Nominal 7.5° buffer group and independently measured narrower-texture group × BHO 0 and 2.7 mol % |
| Scale-up, core | Locked qualified recipe | Selected composition/thickness on 200 m tape; retain contemporaneous undoped witness segments |

Use three independently coated replicates per coupon arm **P**, randomize coating order, and block analyses by tape/precursor lot. Shared reference arms need not be duplicated. Nominal thicknesses are 0.35, 1.4, and approximately 3.1 µm; report actual values. Nine × 0.35 = 3.15 µm, so calibrate the nine-cycle increment to approximately 0.344 µm if targeting 3.1 µm. Do not assign thickness from pass count alone.

Texture-group availability is **U**; without it, report sharpening within the available group, not independence from buffer misalignment. The comparison up to approximately 8.5° requires an additional matched template group if that claim is investigated.

##### 3. Materials, equipment, and consolidated settings
Use qualified fluorine-free Gd/Ba/Cu precursor, base cation ratio 1:2:3; separately qualified colloidal BHO; compatible solvents; CeO2 sputtering target; IBAD/CeO2 tape; clean containers; Ag voltage-pad material and high-current terminals. Record reagent purity and batch quantities calculated from coating yield; no fixed batch volume is assumed.

Required equipment: RF sputterer; reel-to-reel slot die and tension control; separately controlled drying, pyrolysis and crystallization zones; calibrated thermocouples, gas mixing, pressure and oxygen monitoring; DLS plus high-resolution particle imaging; TGA for recipe qualification; XRD; SEM; FIB and TEM/STEM-EDX; thickness metrology; cryogenic DC transport system; rotation stage; THEVA Tapestar.

| Operation | Consolidated setting / acceptance |
|---|---|
| Tape geometry | Nominal 4 mm width **P**; measure actual width, active thickness and tap spacing |
| CeO2 sputtering pilot | 700 °C, 150 W, 10 mTorr total, Ar:O2 = 4:1 **P**; power density/tool compatibility must be verified |
| CeO2 rate/thickness | Calibrate near 0.8 nm/s **P**; added thickness ±5% **P** |
| Slot die pilot | 50 µm gap, 0.5 m/min, 0.1 mL/min **P**; adjust flow to measured crystallized yield |
| Drying | Initial 5 min **P**, temperature/airflow **U**; verify solvent removal without skinning |
| Pyrolysis pilot | 450 °C, 5 °C/min, 30 min **P**, within draft 400–500 °C range; oxidizing atmosphere qualified by TGA/film inspection |
| Crystallization | 800–840 °C; start qualification at 820 °C **P**; low pO2, pressure, dwell, ramp and cooling profile **U** |
| Oxygenation pilot | Final film: 500 °C, 4 h flowing O2 **P**; gas exchange, cooling and oxygenation endpoint qualified |
| Process tolerances | Furnace uniformity ±5 °C; thickness variation ±5% **P**; atmosphere tolerance set from qualified process window |
| Transport stability | ±0.1 K, ±0.01 T, normal-field alignment ±0.5° **P** |

At 4 mm width, the proposed flow/speed give wet thickness `Q/(wv) = 50 µm`, not 0.35 µm. A 0.35 µm final increment implies a thickness yield of 0.007. Calibrate solids concentration, density and shrinkage; a coater specification of ±1 µm cannot establish submicrometre final-film precision.

- Matched composition, thickness, interface and texture comparisons separate the intended variables. Independent replicates, lot blocking and actual thickness measurements preserve comparison traceability; unavailable texture groups limit the conclusions. **Source:** B003, B004, B005, B006.
- The listed resources support coating, atmosphere control and characterization. The proposed coating flow determines wet thickness, not crystallized thickness, so measured yield calibration is necessary before assigning film increments. **Source:** B007, B008, B009, B010.

### Experimental Task 2: Prepare buffered tape and dispersions, qualify conversion and fabricate complete-cycle films

#### Specific Content

##### 4. Sequential fabrication and hand-offs
1. **Receive and inspect tape.** Record stack, width, lot, roll orientation and position. Inspect for cracks/delamination; measure CeO2 thickness, surface morphology and Δϕ before modification. Use supplier-approved solvent cleaning; qualify ultrasound or aqueous exposure on sacrificial tape first. Dry with filtered N2. Avoid unqualified 500 °C pre-anneals or surface polishing.
2. **Modify CeO2.** Calibrate sputter rate on witnesses, pre-sputter behind shutter, and deposit the assigned additional thickness without replacing the existing buffer. Qualify substrate heating and cooling against substrate oxidation and delamination. Measure thickness and texture again; inspect roughness/morphology. **Hand-off H1:** release labeled buffered tape and witness data to coating only if intact and within its assigned texture/thickness group.
3. **Qualify BHO.** Obtain or synthesize colloidal BHO using a documented, experimentally qualified route. Verify primary size 1.6–2.2 nm by suitable high-resolution imaging and phase/composition evidence. DLS screens hydrodynamic aggregation but cannot alone establish primary crystallite size or BHO identity. Do not use bulk calcination/grinding as a presumed route to this size.
4. **Prepare composition batches.** Verify metal concentrations and nanoparticle stock concentration. Pending denominator resolution, the proposed definition is `100 n(BHO)/n(GdBCO formula units)`; explicitly distinguish it from total-metal mol %. Add BHO without changing the base Gd:Ba:Cu ratio. Match solvent and solids across arms, including the undoped solvent control. Use cooled, enclosed sonication; qualify dose and confirm size before/after mixing. Check viscosity, sedimentation and stability over the actual coating interval. Do not oxygen-anneal a liquid precursor. **H2:** release homogeneous, composition-verified batches with recorded particle distributions; quarantine aggregated stocks.
5. **Qualify thermal conversion.** On sacrificial coupons, establish drying/pyrolysis completion, then screen low-pO2 crystallization at 820 °C, with proposed dwell screening spanning 15–120 min. Determine the shortest complete conversion compatible with texture and 2–10 nm particles. Measure pO2 at operating pressure; gas flow alone does not define it. Do not use pure O2 at 1 atm for crystallization. Lock qualified atmosphere and ramp/cooling profiles before comparative fabrication.
6. **Execute complete cycles.** Coat → dry → pyrolyze → crystallize → controlled cool to the qualified handling temperature. Repeat this full sequence for each assigned cycle; do not accumulate all coatings before one crystallization. Record tension, speed, flow, temperatures, pO2 and each earlier layer’s repeated heat exposure. Use clean handling between cycles. Retain sacrificial companions at cycles 1, 4 and 9. Oxygenate the completed film; qualify any additional inter-cycle oxygenation separately.
7. **Final release and allocation.** Inspect and measure final thickness. **H3:** transfer intact parent tapes to transport/mapping; allocate linked adjacent pieces to SEM/XRD and destructive TEM. Record every cut and coordinate. Map parent material before destructive preparation where practical.

#### Related Knowledge

- Buffer modification adds CeO2 without replacing the supplied stack. H1 links tape integrity, measured thickness and texture to the coating release. **Source:** B011.
- Primary BHO size and identity require suitable imaging and phase/composition evidence; DLS only screens hydrodynamic aggregation. H2 releases composition-verified, stable batches while aggregated stocks remain quarantined. **Source:** B011.
- Thermal qualification precedes comparative fabrication, with oxygen partial pressure measured at operating pressure. Repeating complete conversion cycles preserves the specified workflow and records cumulative heat exposure; H3 links released parent tapes to characterization specimens. **Source:** B011.

### Experimental Task 3: Characterize phase, texture, morphology and nanoparticle dispersion

#### Specific Content

##### 5. Structural characterization and gates
Use XRD θ–2θ scans, initially 20–80° 2θ with 0.02° steps **P**, extending the range if necessary for phase identification. Measure GdBCO in-plane FWHM using a validated asymmetric reflection, such as (103), and CeO2 using {111} at the appropriate tilt (approximately 54.7° for a (001) surface). Symmetric CeO2 (002) or GdBCO (005) scans do not measure Δϕ. Use GdBCO (005) rocking curves separately for out-of-plane spread. Record wavelength, reflection, tilt, background/fit and instrumental broadening.

Target GdBCO Δϕ approximately 2–3° by approximately 0.35 µm. This does not directly measure the grain-boundary angle distribution; inspect local boundaries before asserting low-angle boundaries.

SEM: image representative start/middle/end regions, initially at 5 kV **P**; quantify cracks, precipitates and coverage. Cross-sectional microscopy establishes thickness and interfaces. TEM: use protected FIB lamellae, e.g. 30 kV milling followed by 5 kV finishing **P**. Begin STEM-EDX at 200 kV **P**, adjusting dose to avoid damage. Sample multiple depths and interfaces; record analyzed area, particle count and sampling uncertainty. Target film particles 2–10 nm; map Hf/Ba and inspect intragranular versus boundary locations. EDX alone does not prove perovskite identity or resolve every 2 nm particle.

**H4:** pass intact morphology, qualified phase conversion and dispersion. Quarantine failures; retain their measurements as failed-arm data, not silently excluded results.

#### Related Knowledge

- Validated asymmetric reflections measure in-plane texture, whereas symmetric scans do not establish in-plane FWHM. The film-texture target does not itself establish a local grain-boundary angle distribution. **Source:** B012, B013.
- SEM and cross-sectional microscopy address morphology, thickness and interfaces. TEM/STEM-EDX sampling addresses particle size and location with recorded sampling uncertainty, but EDX alone cannot prove BHO perovskite identity or resolve every small particle. **Source:** B014.
- H4 requires intact morphology, qualified conversion and dispersion. Quarantining failures does not remove their measurements from the comparison record. **Source:** B015.

### Experimental Task 4: Measure full-width transport and angular response, normalize results and map qualified scale-up tape

#### Specific Content

##### 6. Transport, mapping and consistency checks
Prepare robust current terminals outside voltage taps; qualify contact attachment on witnesses without abrasive removal of GdBCO. Thin Ag pads may serve voltage sensing but are not presumed adequate current leads. Verify terminal heating and current rating. Use measured 10 mm voltage-tap spacing **P**: the 1 µV/cm criterion gives `Vc = 1 µV`. A 1 mm bridge spacing instead requires 0.1 µV.

For each released arm, measure full-width Ic at 77 K self-field and 20 K at proposed fields 0, 1, 4, 8, 12 and 18 T. Use B normal to the tape as the proposed comparison orientation, verifying the c-axis/normal offset mechanically and by diffraction; the supplied high-field reference orientation remains unspecified. Zero applied field is not zero self-field.

Verify 18 T operation at 20 K and sample-holder bore compatibility; a 12 T system cannot perform this endpoint. Qualify a supply/current path of at least 600 A **P** for testing near or above 500 A. A 100 A sweep cannot test 467 A, and a 500 A system cannot establish >500 A. If limited, report `Ic > Imax` only when the criterion is not reached.

Stabilize field/temperature; acquire stepped DC V–I curves with settling, paired positive/negative readings for offset cancellation, and repeat sweeps. Use adaptive steps near the criterion; stop on heating, runaway voltage or equipment limits. Establish abort limits and safe ramp rates before testing. Report noise, Ic uncertainty and repeatability; investigate >3% repeat differences **P**.

At 20 K and 77 K, measure angular Ic at 8 T, initially 0–90° in 5° steps **P**, with B rotated transverse to current. Record angle zero and rotation plane; do not presume where maxima occur. Smaller bridges are optional diagnostic witnesses, not replacements for full-width results.

Compute `Jc = Ic/(wt)` using superconducting film thickness. For 4 mm × 3.1 µm, area is `1.24×10^-4 cm²`; 171 A corresponds to `1.38×10^6 A/cm²`. A 50 µm bridge would carry approximately 2.14 A at equal Jc, but patterning damage must be checked. Normalize width only with an explicit uniformity assumption. For B perpendicular to current, `Fp = Jc B` in N/m³ using SI units—not `Ic B`.

Calibrate THEVA Tapestar against matched 77 K DC coupons and verify mapping temperature, spatial response and conversion uncertainty. Qualify the proposed 5 m/min, 5 mm sampling interval; this requires at least 16.7 samples/s and a 40 min scan for 200 m. Sampling interval is not demonstrated spatial resolution. After locking the selected recipe, fabricate and map 200 m, recording coordinate, mean, minimum, standard deviation, CV and defects. Compare with the supplied 467 A/4-mm average without claiming reproduction in advance.

#### Related Knowledge

- The electric-field criterion must be converted using measured voltage-tap spacing. Current terminals, supply capacity and verified field capability determine which endpoints can actually be measured. **Source:** B016, B017, B018.
- Stable conditions, offset-cancelled repeated sweeps and documented angular geometry support transport comparisons. Jc uses measured superconducting cross-section, and perpendicular-field pinning force uses Jc rather than Ic. **Source:** B019, B020, B021.
- Longitudinal mapping requires calibration against matched DC coupons and qualification of scan speed, sampling and uncertainty. Sampling interval is not established spatial resolution, and the supplied long-length current remains a reference target. **Source:** B022.

### Experimental Task 5: Record failures, apply controlled feedback and archive core and optional analyses

#### Specific Content

##### 7. Disposition, safety and controlled feedback
Aggregation: quarantine, adjust compatible dispersion conditions, remeasure concentration/size. Residual carbon/BaCO3: review pyrolysis, atmosphere and phase evidence; requalify rather than increasing oxygen blindly. Coarsening: shorten validated crystallization exposure; do not use an unsupported temperature×time ceiling. Cracking/delamination: examine drying, incremental thickness, tension and thermal ramps. Low Ic: distinguish contact heating, thickness error, oxygenation, texture and boundary segregation before changing chemistry.

Use solvent exhaust, nanoparticle containment, oxidizer-compatible gas systems, furnace interlocks, cryogen ventilation/oxygen-deficiency monitoring, magnet exclusion zones and quench procedures. High-current fixtures need insulated leads, thermal monitoring and emergency shutdown.

Archive raw spectra/images/V–I curves, travelers, calibration records, exclusions, uncertainty and longitudinal coordinates. Analyze matched arms before selecting scale-up conditions. Change one diagnosed factor per calibration round, retain unchanged controls, and freeze the recipe before confirmatory comparisons.

Optional extensions: direct 20 T testing only on verified equipment; otherwise label any extrapolation and uncertainty. Model-dependent Kramer analysis may use `Jc^(1/2) B^(1/4)` versus B, but cannot independently establish pinning mechanism or irreversibility field without validating its assumptions.

#### Related Knowledge

- Failure disposition follows diagnosis of aggregation, conversion, coarsening, mechanical damage or low current. Changes require requalification rather than unsupported atmosphere or thermal adjustments. **Source:** B023.
- Solvent, nanoparticle, oxidizer, furnace, cryogenic, magnet and high-current safeguards are prerequisites for the corresponding operations. **Source:** B024.
- Archived raw data and calibration records support matched-arm analysis before recipe selection. Controlled calibration retains unchanged controls; optional direct 20 T testing and model-dependent analysis remain separate from core measurements. **Source:** B025, B026.

## Refined Experimental Plan

### 1. Release prerequisites and comparison allocation

Approve a traveler covering the purchased IBAD/CeO2 stack, BHO loading denominator, precursor and solvent chemistry, nanoparticle route, atmosphere and validated thermal profiles. Assign lot, loading, added-buffer thickness, measured texture group, cycle count, replicate and longitudinal position identifiers. ### 2. Qualified preparation and complete-cycle fabrication
Inspect the supplied tape and measure width, buffer thickness, morphology and texture. Use approved cleaning; qualify potentially damaging treatments on sacrificial tape. Calibrate RF sputtering before adding the assigned CeO2 thickness. The 700 °C, 150 W, 10 mTorr, Ar:O2 4:1 pilot and approximately 0.8 nm/s rate are **P**, not released recipes. **H1:** release only intact tape within its assigned measured group.

Verify precursor concentrations and the base Gd:Ba:Cu ratio of 1:2:3. Qualify colloidal BHO identity and 1.6–2.2 nm primary size using imaging and phase/composition evidence; DLS alone is insufficient. Resolve the loading denominator; the proposed definition is 100 n(BHO)/n(GdBCO formula units). Match solvents and solids, including undoped controls, and qualify enclosed cooled sonication and coating-interval stability. **H2:** release verified homogeneous batches; quarantine aggregation.

Calibrate coating yield rather than equating wet thickness with final film thickness. The slot-die settings are **P**; report actual thickness instead of assigning it from cycle count. On sacrificial coupons, qualify drying and pyrolysis, including the 450 °C, 5 °C/min, 30 min **P** pilot. Qualify crystallization within 800–840 °C, starting at 820 °C **P** with proposed 15–120 min dwell screening. Exact low pO2, pressure, ramps, dwell and cooling remain **U** until qualified. Measure pO2 at operating pressure; gas flow is not a substitute, and pure O2 at 1 atm is excluded for crystallization.

Lock conversion conditions before comparisons. Repeat coat–dry–pyrolyze–crystallize–controlled cool for every cycle, recording cumulative thermal exposure. Retain companions at cycles one, four and nine. Qualify completed-film oxygenation using the 500 °C, four-hour flowing-O2 pilot **P**; additional inter-cycle oxygenation requires separate qualification. **H3:** release intact measured films and linked adjacent characterization pieces.

### 3. Structural gates and transport
Use XRD phase scans, validated asymmetric in-plane texture measurements and separate rocking curves. The approximately 2–3° film-texture target is not a grain-boundary distribution measurement. Use SEM and cross-sectional TEM/STEM-EDX to examine coverage, cracks, interfaces and the 2–10 nm film-particle target, recording sampling uncertainty and identity limitations. **H4:** quarantine failed morphology, conversion or dispersion while retaining failed-arm data.

Qualify terminals, heating, current capacity and safe abort limits. Measure full-width Ic at 77 K self-field and 20 K at proposed 0/1/4/8/12/18 T, with normal-field comparison geometry. Verify 18 T capability and at least 600 A capacity **P** where needed. Apply 1 µV/cm using measured tap spacing. Stabilize conditions, cancel offsets, repeat sweeps and report uncertainty; use censored Ic > Imax only when the criterion is unreached. Measure angular Ic at 8 T at both temperatures. Calculate Jc from measured superconducting width and thickness and Fp = Jc B in SI units. Optional bridges do not replace full-width results.

### 4. Scale-up, disposition and records
Select the locked scale-up recipe only after matched-arm analysis. Retain undoped witnesses, calibrate Tapestar against DC coupons, qualify 5 m/min and 5 mm sampling **P**, and map 200 m with coordinates, statistics and defects. The supplied 171 A and 467 A values remain reference targets; 162 A at 20 T is an extrapolated reference. Direct 20 T testing and Kramer analysis are optional, subject to equipment and assumption limitations.

Diagnose failures, change one factor per calibration round and retain controls. Archive raw data, travelers, uncertainties, exclusions and coordinates. Require solvent exhaust, nanoparticle containment, oxidizer-compatible systems, furnace interlocks, cryogenic ventilation and oxygen-deficiency monitoring, magnet/quench controls, and protected high-current fixtures with emergency shutdown.
