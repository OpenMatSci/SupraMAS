# Experimental Plan: Embedded BaCeO3/CeO2 pinning centers in REBCO coated conductors

## Experimental Draft

### Stage 1 — Qualify resources and prepare composite powders
Evaluate whether embedded BaCeO3/CeO2 inclusions improve YBCO transport Jc(B,T) without compromising connectivity or texture. Phase retention, interface coherence and alignment remain hypotheses. Compare vehicle/sonication-matched undoped films with composite arms annealed at 800, 900 and 1000 °C in three independent synthesis/deposition blocks. Split each block's precursor across the anneals, use one matrix-ink batch per block, and randomize coating and measurement order.

Use Ba/Ce nitrates, ammonia, peroxide and qualified Y:Ba:Cu = 1:2:3 ink. Qualify buffered Hastelloy/MgO/LaMnO3 tape, reactor, thermal and atmosphere controls, characterization instruments and cryogenic transport capacity before release. Reverse-precipitate into a vented alkaline bath, quantify Ba/Ce recovery, and qualify both intended phases after the specified air anneals and programmed cooling. Incomplete Ba recovery must be quantified; CeO2-only material is not a qualified composite.

### Stage 2 — Fabricate and structurally qualify coated conductors
Disperse released powders in matched inks and spin-coat the qualified tape. Nominal 5 vol% additive loading, 350 nm thickness and unspecified operating settings are proposed choices requiring pilot qualification before matrix lock. Qualify pyrolysis, growth and oxygenation for the selected ink. Use Co-source XRD, Raman, DTA/TG-MS and microscopy to assess phases, carbonate detection limits, distinct domain/particle sizes, texture and embedded interfaces.

### Stage 3 — Measure transport and consolidate evidence
Release films before bridge patterning. Measure four-terminal transport using the fixed 1 μV/cm criterion, primarily at 20–50 K and 1–5 T, with 77 K references. Normalize by measured dimensions and compare paired composite/undoped results within blocks. Anticipated outputs are traceable qualification decisions, structural records and transport comparisons—not guaranteed enhancement. Apply chemical, nanopowder, furnace, oxygen, magnet and cryogen safeguards throughout; quarantine failures and keep extensions separate from the core matrix.

## Detailed Experimental Tasks

### Experimental Task 1: Stage 1 — Qualify materials, equipment and comparison settings

#### Specific Content

##### 1. Scope and comparison matrix
Evaluate whether carbonate-free, nanoscale BaCeO3/CeO2 inclusions improve REBCO transport Jc(B,T), while preserving current connectivity and texture. Phase retention, interface coherence and particle alignment are hypotheses to test, not assumed outcomes.

**Status convention:** Task-fixed conditions are identified below. All other numerical operating settings are **proposed, requiring calibration**. Complete pilot qualification before locking the comparison matrix.

Use YBCO as the fixed REBCO composition, on one lot of qualified Hastelloy/MgO/LaMnO3 buffered tape. Retain and document any underlying diffusion-barrier layers in the qualified stack; do not substitute single-crystal substrates.

| Arm | Powder treatment | Film additive |
|---|---|---|
| U | None | Undoped, vehicle/sonication matched |
| C800 | 800 °C | BaCeO3/CeO2 composite |
| C900 | 900 °C | BaCeO3/CeO2 composite |
| C1000 | 1000 °C | BaCeO3/CeO2 composite |

Run three independent blocks, R01–R03. Within each block split one synthesized precursor batch across the three anneals; prepare all films from one matrix-ink batch. IDs: `R01-C800-P` powder, `-I` ink, `-F01` transport film, `-W01` adjacent structural witness; analogous IDs for other arms. Randomize coating and measurement order. Multiple bridges are technical repeats, not independent replicates.

Fix nominal final oxide additive loading at 5 vol% across composite arms and nominal film thickness at 350 nm. Calculate loading from measured phase fractions and validated phase densities, not liquid volume. The core matrix varies powder annealing temperature only; lock all other settings.

##### 2. Materials, equipment and release prerequisites
Use Ba(NO3)2 and Ce(NO3)3·6H2O, aqueous ammonia, H2O2, DI water, dilute HNO3 only if needed for dissolution, and a qualified Y:Ba:Cu = 1:2:3 precursor ink. Organic solvents in the film ink do not constitute organic powder precipitants. Record certificates, stock assays and lot numbers.

Required equipment: ventilated stirred reactor, calibrated pH/temperature probes and dosing pump; centrifuge with known rotor radius; drying oven; air furnace rated beyond 1000 °C with sample thermocouple; ultrasonic homogenizer; spin coater and tape fixture; controlled-pO2/humidity growth furnace; Co-source diffractometer; 532 nm Raman; DTA/TG-MS; FIB-SEM and TEM/STEM-EDS; thickness metrology; four-terminal cryogenic transport system with nominal 9 T magnet, nanovoltmeter and suitable external current source.

Before release, verify furnace uniformity, actual cooling trajectory, gas composition at the specimen, complete buffer-stack integrity, ink compatibility, bridge fabrication damage and cryogenic wiring/current capacity. A nominal instrument specification is not qualification.

##### 3. Consolidated operating settings

| Operation | Setpoint and tolerance/status |
|---|---|
| Powder anneal | **Task:** 800/900/1000 °C, 60 min, cooling 10 °C/min; proposed acceptance ±5 °C, ±2 min, ±1 °C/min |
| Powder heating | Air; proposed 5 ±0.5 °C/min |
| Precipitation pilot | Proposed feed: separate 50 mL, 0.1 mol/L Ba and Ce nitrate solutions; Ba:Ce feed 1:1 |
| Reverse addition | Proposed alkaline bath 100 mL; pH 10.7 ±0.2; 25 ±2 °C; feed 1.0 ±0.1 mL/min; approximately 1000 rpm, no splashing |
| Oxidant | Proposed H2O2:Ce molar ratio 1:1 using freshly assayed, diluted peroxide; quantify dose, not ambiguous “vol%” |
| Recovery/drying | Proposed 5000 ×g, 10 min; three DI-water washes; 80 °C, ≥6 h to constant mass |
| Ink dispersion | Proposed 20 kHz, 10 min, 5 s on/2 s off, <25 °C; determine delivered J/mL calorimetrically |
| Coating | Proposed 500 rpm/5 s then 2000 rpm/30 s; 80 °C/5 min drying; calibrated coat count |
| Pyrolysis | Proposed 2 °C/min to 400 °C, 2 h, humid O2; pH2O = 0.1 atm |
| Growth | Proposed 5 °C/min to 790 ±5 °C, 30 ±2 min; pO2 = (1.0 ±0.2)×10⁻³ atm; humidity/flow qualified for selected ink |
| Oxygenation | Proposed cool 10 °C/min to 500 °C, then 5 h in O2 at total pressure 1 atm; controlled O2 cooling |
| Transport | Proposed T = 20, 30, 40, 50, 77 K; B = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 T; T stability ±0.1 K; field calibration target ±1% |

Do not use sealed hydrothermal processing, a 600 °C-only calcination, reducing anneals or liquid-nitrogen quenching in the core route.

#### Related Knowledge

- The comparison isolates powder annealing temperature through matched undoped and composite arms, independent blocks, shared matrix ink within each block and randomized order. Multiple bridges provide technical repeats rather than additional independent replicates. **Source:** B003, B004, B005.
- The coated-conductor architecture requires one qualified buffered-tape lot with its underlying diffusion barriers retained. Equipment ratings alone do not establish furnace, atmosphere, buffer, ink or transport readiness. **Source:** B002, B007, B008.

### Experimental Task 2: Stage 1 — Reverse-precipitate, recover and document the precursor

#### Specific Content

##### A. Reverse precipitation and recovery
1. Prepare and mix nitrate feeds. Use the minimum dilute acid needed for clear dissolution; record acid consumption. Prepare a covered, vented alkaline bath using fresh DI water and ammonia, minimizing atmospheric CO2 exposure without sealing the vessel.
2. Meter **metal feed into the alkaline bath**, maintaining pH through separate ammonia dosing. Meter diluted peroxide separately; never premix concentrated peroxide with ammonia. Record temperature, pH, doses and visible gas evolution continuously. Stir a further proposed 30 min after feed completion.
3. Recover solids. Retain mother liquor and every wash for Ba/Ce elemental analysis by a validated method. Wash until soluble nitrate/ammonium removal meets a pilot-established conductivity endpoint. Do not acid-wash routinely: this can leach Ba and alter the intended mixture.
4. Dry to constant mass, gently homogenize and split into three labeled aliquots. Reserve unannealed material for thermal analysis. Record mass balance and Ba/Ce recovery.

**Gate A:** Ba hydroxide precipitation is not assured because soluble Ba can remain in this chemistry. Confirm recoverable Ba-containing precursor and subsequent dual-phase formation; a CeO2-only product does not qualify as the core composite. Adjust bath chemistry only in pilots, then repeat the full block.

**Hand-off:** synthesis operator transfers sealed powder aliquots, retained liquors and complete chemistry log to thermal/analysis operators.

#### Related Knowledge

- Reverse addition means metering metal feed into the alkaline bath, with separate ammonia and diluted-peroxide dosing. Retained mother liquor and washes support the Ba/Ce recovery balance; routine acid washing can alter the intended mixture through Ba leaching. **Source:** B013.
- Ba precipitation is unresolved in this chemistry. Gate A requires evidence of recoverable Ba-containing precursor and subsequent dual-phase formation; a CeO2-only product cannot enter the qualified composite comparison. **Source:** B014.
- The chemistry log, labeled aliquots and retained liquors preserve the synthesis-to-analysis hand-off and allow recovery failures to be diagnosed. **Source:** B013, B015.

### Experimental Task 3: Stage 1 — Anneal and release qualified composite powders

#### Specific Content

##### B. Anneal and powder qualification
5. Place aliquots in clean covered alumina crucibles with equal, shallow powder-bed geometry. Run the specified anneals in air; start the 60 min dwell when the specimen thermocouple reaches tolerance. Cool at 10 °C/min to room temperature where actively controllable; document any low-temperature deviation.
6. Store dry and sealed. Analyze each annealed powder as described below.

**Gate B:** both intended phases identified; each coherent-domain size ≤35 nm; physical particles <40 nm within measurement uncertainty; carbonate below experimentally established XRD/Raman detection limits. Report size distributions and detection limits, not absolute purity. Quarantine failures; retain them for diagnosis, but exclude them from qualified performance comparisons.

**Hand-off:** release powder mass, phase fractions, particle statistics and carbonate assessment to ink preparation.

#### Related Knowledge

- Equal shallow powder beds and specimen-temperature-based dwell timing support comparable anneals. The programmed cooling trajectory must be documented, including low-temperature deviations. **Source:** B010, B016.
- Gate B distinguishes coherent-domain size from physical particle size and requires both intended phases plus carbonate assessment against established detection limits. Failed powders are quarantined rather than treated as qualified comparisons. **Source:** B017.
- Released phase fractions, particle statistics and carbonate assessment provide the powder information needed for subsequent ink preparation and oxide-loading calculation. **Source:** B005, B018.

### Experimental Task 4: Stage 2 — Prepare matched inks and fabricate qualified YBCO films

#### Specific Content

##### C. Buffered tape, ink and film
7. Obtain qualified buffered tapes from one lot. Inspect optical/SEM images and XRD texture; proposed in-plane FWHM gate <5°. Use solvent cleaning validated not to damage the cap layer. If fabricating buffers locally, qualify the complete IBAD-MgO/LaMnO3 stack under the laboratory SOP before this experiment; unverified direct MgO deposition on bare Hastelloy is not an acceptable shortcut.
8. Prepare matched inks, incorporating released powder at 5 vol% final oxide loading. Apply equal solvent adjustment and sonication to U. Measure viscosity, hydrodynamic aggregation and sedimentation over the actual coating interval. DLS does not measure crystallite size. Proposed dispersion gate: aggregate D50 <50 nm and no measurable concentration drift during coating, validated against microscopy and elemental sampling.
9. Spin-coat mounted tape coupons using the locked settings. Calibrate coat count beforehand to produce 350 ±50 nm films; log deposited volume and all intermediate drying steps. Do not introduce surfactants only into selected arms.
10. Execute pyrolysis, epitaxial growth and oxygenation using the qualified schedule. Monitor exhaust and gas conditions. The proposed schedule cannot be transferred between precursor formulations without qualification.
11. Measure thickness, inspect cracks/delamination and characterize witnesses. Pattern transport bridges only after film release.

**Gate C:** continuous film; no through-bridge cracks; qualified texture; embedded inclusions rather than a surface particle layer; no resolved continuous interfacial reaction layer. Recheck particle sizes after growth. Record exclusions before viewing transport comparisons.

**Hand-off:** film operator supplies transport specimens and adjacent witnesses with thickness maps, atmosphere histories and gate decisions.

#### Related Knowledge

- Qualified buffered tape, validated cleaning and matched solvent/sonication treatment preserve the comparison. DLS characterizes hydrodynamic aggregation, not crystallite size, and dispersion qualification addresses the actual coating interval. **Source:** B019.
- Coat-count calibration and an ink-specific qualified pyrolysis, growth and oxygenation schedule precede film release. A proposed schedule cannot be transferred between precursor formulations without qualification. **Source:** B010, B019.
- Gate C requires continuous, textured films with embedded inclusions, no through-bridge cracks and no resolved continuous interfacial reaction layer. Exclusions are recorded before transport comparisons are viewed. **Source:** B020, B021.

### Experimental Task 5: Stage 2 — Characterize powder phases, thermal behavior and film microstructure

#### Specific Content

##### 5. Structural and thermal characterization
- **XRD:** Co Kα, proposed powder 2θ = 10–120°, 0.02° steps, 2 s/step; extend counting for trace phases. Verify tube settings against instrument ratings and calibrate wavelength/line profile with standards. Do not reuse Cu-source peak angles. Calculate reflection positions by Bragg’s law from validated structures and the actual wavelength; distinguish Kα1 from the doublet treatment.
- Refine BaCeO3 polymorphs, CeO2 and plausible carbonate/secondary phases by Rietveld analysis. Check residuals and alternative models; fit indices alone do not prove phase identity. For Williamson–Hall use instrumental-corrected breadth β in **radians**: βcosθ = Kλ/D + 4εsinθ. Use multiple resolved reflections separately for each phase; report overlap and uncertainty. Use consistent length units for λ and D.
- For film texture use symmetric REBCO (00l), such as (005), for out-of-plane rocking curves; use an accessible asymmetric reflection such as (103) for φ-scans. A (005) φ-scan cannot establish biaxial texture.
- **Raman:** 532 nm; proposed 100–1200 cm⁻¹, 30 s ×5 acquisitions at ≥5 locations. Calibrate with Si; begin below 1 mW and increase only after verifying no power-dependent changes. Include the carbonate stretching region near 1050–1060 cm⁻¹. Establish matrix-specific limits with carbonate-spiked standards.
- **DTA/TG-MS:** task-fixed 10 K/min in air; proposed room temperature–1000 °C, approximately 20 mg, 100 mL/min air, subject to instrument calibration. Run empty-pan and gas blanks; monitor H2O, CO2 and nitrogen-oxide channels with interference corrections. CO2 evolution alone does not identify BaCO3.
- **FIB-SEM/TEM:** inspect ≥5 separated regions. Protect lift-outs with Pt; coarse mill at 30 kV, finish at 5 then 2 kV after damage qualification. Obtain <80 nm lamellae where feasible. Use STEM-EDS plus diffraction/HRTEM to distinguish phases, measure inclusions, clustering, orientation and reaction layers. Report actual particle counts; target ≥100 inclusions per arm. Calibrate strain mapping against an unstrained reference. Perform destructive microscopy on witnesses or after transport.

#### Related Knowledge

- Co-source diffraction requires wavelength-specific reflection positions and calibrated instrumental broadening. Rietveld model checks and phase-specific Williamson–Hall analysis support phase and coherent-domain assessment without treating fit indices as proof of identity. **Source:** B022.
- Raman carbonate assessment uses matrix-specific detection limits, while DTA/TG-MS uses blanks and interference corrections. CO2 evolution alone does not identify BaCO3. **Source:** B022.
- Film texture requires appropriate symmetric and asymmetric reflections. Witness microscopy combines elemental and crystallographic evidence to examine embedded phases, clustering, orientation and interfaces, with reported counts and qualified preparation damage. **Source:** B022.

### Experimental Task 6: Stage 3 — Pattern bridges and qualify electrical measurement capacity

#### Specific Content

##### 6. Transport and equipment consistency
Pattern a proposed 50 ±2 μm-wide bridge with **1.00 ±0.02 mm voltage-tap spacing**; current pads lie outside the taps. Measure actual width and superconducting thickness locally. Validate Ag contacts without a post-contact heat treatment unless its effect on oxygenation has been qualified.

At 350 nm thickness, cross-section is 1.75×10⁻⁷ cm². Thus Jc = 1 MA/cm² requires Ic = 0.175 A; 4 MA/cm² requires 0.700 A. A Keithley 6221 has a nominal 0.100 A maximum, insufficient even for the first value. Qualify an external source, leads, feedthroughs, contact heating and protection; lower-temperature currents may exceed 1 A. If capacity is insufficient, redesign geometry before locking all arms or report a censored lower bound.

The fixed 1 μV/cm criterion gives **Vc = 0.100 μV = 100 nV** for 1 mm taps. Verify offset/noise below proposed 10 nV and calibrate low-voltage response. Require contact dissipation I²Rc to remain below the experimentally qualified thermal budget, rather than accepting Rc <1 Ω generically.

#### Related Knowledge

- Actual bridge width and local superconducting thickness determine current-density normalization. Contact processing must not introduce an unqualified change to oxygenation. **Source:** B023, B027.
- The source's current examples are equipment-sizing references, not measured performance. External-source capacity, wiring, contact heating and protection must be qualified; insufficient capacity requires pre-lock geometry redesign or a censored lower bound. **Source:** B024.
- The fixed electric-field criterion produces a 100 nV threshold for 1 mm voltage taps. Low-voltage response and offsets must be checked, and contact dissipation must remain within an experimentally qualified thermal budget. **Source:** B025.

### Experimental Task 7: Stage 3 — Acquire transport curves and calculate normalized quantities

#### Specific Content

Mount with thermal anchoring; align B perpendicular to tape, approximately parallel to REBCO c. Verify actual temperature and magnet capability. At each setpoint equilibrate, sweep current with fine steps around the criterion, reverse polarity to remove offsets, and acquire three repeat sweeps. Compare DC/pulsed sweeps during qualification to exclude heating; lock the waveform thereafter. Abort for temperature drift, runaway voltage or contact heating.

Calculate Jc = Ic/(wt), with dimensional uncertainty. Compute Fp = JcB in N/m³ using Jc in A/m² and B in tesla. Report zero applied field separately as self-field. Do not mix H in A/m with B in tesla.

#### Related Knowledge

- Thermal anchoring, actual temperature verification, field alignment, polarity reversal and repeated sweeps support transport acquisition. Waveform qualification addresses heating, with abort conditions for drift, runaway voltage or contact heating. **Source:** B026.
- Jc uses measured cross-sectional dimensions and their uncertainty. Pinning-force density uses Jc in A/m² and B in tesla; zero applied field is reported separately as self-field. **Source:** B027.

### Experimental Task 8: Stage 3 — Analyze comparisons, disposition failures and control extensions

#### Specific Content

##### 7. Analysis, disposition and controlled feedback
Record raw files, specimen lineage, masses, assays, calibrations, timestamps, furnace/gas traces, dimensions, contact checks, imaging locations, exclusions and analysis scripts. Compare paired composite/U Jc ratios within blocks at identical B,T; report between-block variability and propagated uncertainty without assuming ±5% accuracy.

Carbonate failure: check CO2 exposure, reagents and conversion; reprocess only as a separately identified pilot. Coarsening: reassess thermal uniformity and lower the anneal within 800–1000 °C. Clustering: adjust calibrated dispersion energy/solvent compatibility, not composition mid-matrix. Poor Jc: diagnose bridge damage, contacts, oxygenation, texture and reaction layers before changing loading. Never lower the electric-field criterion to rescue a result. Log each change and repeat affected controls and arms.

Optional extensions, after the core matrix: matched slow-cooling controls with all other settings fixed; high-temperature XRD through 1000 °C and cooling to test polymorph retention; angular Jc and fields up to verified 9 T. Extract Birr at the predeclared operational Jc = 100 A/cm² only if crossed; otherwise report Birr above the accessible field. Normalize pinning curves only with independently determined Birr; fits do not uniquely establish a mechanism.

##### 8. Risk controls
Handle toxic Ba salts, ammonia, acids and peroxide in a hood with splash protection; segregate oxidizers and flammables. Keep peroxide-bearing mixtures vented and verify peroxide removal before contact with organic ink. Collect Ba/Ce-bearing liquid and nanopowder waste separately. Enclose dry-powder handling. Use furnace interlocks, hot-sample tools, exhaust scrubbing appropriate to nitrate/fluorinated ink decomposition, oxygen-service components, magnet exclusion zones and cryogen oxygen-deficiency controls.

#### Related Knowledge

- Paired composite/undoped ratios are compared within blocks at identical field and temperature, with between-block variability and propagated uncertainty supported by traceable raw records. **Source:** B028.
- Failure diagnosis and changes remain controlled and documented. Reprocessing is separately identified as pilot work, affected controls and arms are repeated, and the electric-field criterion is not lowered to rescue a result. **Source:** B029.
- Optional cooling, high-temperature diffraction and expanded transport studies follow the core matrix. Irreversibility-field extraction and pinning normalization are conditional, and fits do not uniquely establish a mechanism. **Source:** B030.
- Chemical, nanopowder, thermal, oxygen-service, magnetic-field and cryogenic risk controls are prerequisites throughout execution, including vented peroxide handling and verified peroxide removal before organic-ink contact. **Source:** B031.
